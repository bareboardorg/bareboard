Exploded View: Sunrise Quickie Q700 M

This page is different, and the difference is the story. Sunrise Medical does not publish an Illustrated Parts Breakdown or a parts catalogue for the Quickie Q700 M — not for owners, not openly at all. Their service instructions say it in one line: “For spare parts please refer to separate manuals.” No exploded views, no part numbers, no way to order a caster bearing without a dealer in the middle.

Coverage: manual-only. What Sunrise does publish is the labeled overview in the Instructions for Use — Fig. 3.1, eleven numbered parts, drawn once and used for all three drive layouts in the family: mid-wheel (Q700 M, top), front-wheel (Q700 F, middle), and rear-wheel (Q700 R, bottom). That is everything the maker gives the person who rides in it. We reproduce that diagram exactly, and we name the gap exactly: every assembly-level breakdown for this chair is a source gap until Sunrise publishes one. The Q700 M is the mid-wheel-drive member of the family — the big drive wheels sit under the seat, in the middle, with a small castor at each corner.

The sources

Guide 1: Instructions for Use — Quickie Q700 M/F/R, document 000690770.02, Revision E (2021), Sunrise Medical
Full guide (PDF, in this site’s library): sunrise_quickie-q700-mfr_instructions-rev-e.pdf

Guide 2: Service Instructions — Quickie Q400 / Q500 / Q700 F/R, document 000690852.01, Revision B, Sunrise Medical (40 pages).
Full guide (PDF, in this site’s library): sunrise_quickie-q400-q500-q700-fr_service-instructions_000690852-rev-b.pdf (also posted on Sunrise Medical’s own document asset bank at sunrisedice.com/asset-bank/assetfile/52975.pdf). It covers the F and R bases only — it says so on its own cover — not the M base.

Guide 3: Q7ME Swing Arm Installation — document 248013, Revision A (May 2018), Sunrise Medical (US) LLC. The one service document found that is written for the Q700M base itself: removing and refitting the rear swing arm (the caster arm), with torque figures.
Full sheet (PDF, in this site’s library): sunrise_quickie-q700m_swing-arm-installation_248013-rev-a.pdf

A newer Revision G of the Instructions for Use exists (it adds the Q700 M HD and the standing Q700-UP M / Q700-UP F to the same manual). This page is built from Revision E, the edition held in the library.

The maker’s own warnings, word for word

Sunrise prints these in the Q700 M/F/R Instructions for Use (Rev. E) and in the Q400/Q500/Q700 F/R Service Instructions (Rev. B). Word for word — including the one that explains why this page cannot give you part numbers:

For spare parts please refer to separate manuals. Please only use genuine spare parts supplied by Sunrise Medical, failure to do so may result in the invalidation of your warranty.

The intended lifetime of the wheelchair is 5 years. Please DO NOT use or fit any 3rd party components to the wheelchair unless they are officially approved by Sunrise Medical.

Follow the instructions carefully next to these warning symbols! Not paying careful attention to these instructions could result in physical injury or damage to the wheelchair or the environment.

The signs, symbols and instructions affixed to the wheelchair comprise part of the safety facilities. They must never be covered or removed. They must remain present and clearly legible throughout the entire lifespan of the wheelchair.

Avoid physical contact with the wheelchair’s motors at all times. Motors are continuously in motion during use and can reach high temperatures. After use, the motors will cool down slowly. Physical contact could cause burns. Allow the motors after using at least 30 min. to cool down.

Do not fit car batteries to the wheelchair. Fit only deep cycle, maintenance free mobility batteries.

Be extra cautious to reduce the risk of dropping a metal tool onto the battery. It could spark or short circuit the battery or other electrical parts that may cause an explosion.

Verbatim from the Instructions for Use, sections 1.1, 2.0, 2.1, 2.2 and 6.1; the spare-parts rule from the Service Instructions, section 4.0.

Build order

The assemblies are laid out in the order you would build the chair: frame first, batteries last. Every step is one labeled diagram from the maker’s guide, followed by its parts table. The Callout column is the number printed on the diagram. There is no part-number column on this page — Sunrise publishes no part numbers for this chair, and we do not invent them.

  • Frame: Step 1: The Q700 M, F, and R, labeled by Sunrise

Stage 1 — Frame

Start with the skeleton. Every other system bolts to the frame, so it goes together first. The three photographs are the same eleven parts on the three drive layouts: mid-wheel on top, front-wheel in the middle, rear-wheel at the bottom. On the Q700 M, the drive wheels are the big wheels under the seat; the small wheels at the front and back corners are castors.

Step 1 — The Q700 M, F, and R, labeled by Sunrise

Part (maker’s label)What this part does
1. HeadrestSupports your head. It adjusts so your head rests in a comfortable position.
2. JoystickThe hand control you drive with: push it the way you want to go. It is the joystick module of the chair’s R-net control system — what its fault codes mean is on this site.
3. Arm RestSupports your arms, and helps you transfer in and out of the chair.
4. Seat CushionThe cushion you sit on, part of the Sedeo seating system.
5. Leg RestSupports your legs. Powered versions tilt to change their angle.
6. Front CastorThe small swivel wheel at the front corner. It is not powered — it follows where the chair turns.
7. Drive WheelThe big powered wheel. The motor turns this one. On the Q700 M it sits under the seat, in the middle — that is what mid-wheel drive means.
8. Rear CastorThe small swivel wheel at the back corner. It is not powered — it follows where the chair turns and keeps the chair steady.
9. MotorsTwo electric motors, one for each drive wheel. They get hot in use — see the maker’s warning above, and give them 30 minutes to cool before you touch them.
10. Battery BoxThe box that holds the two 12-volt batteries. Together they make the 24 volts the chair runs on — the battery lesson is on this site.
11. Back UpholsteryThe fabric back of the seat. It supports your back.

The chair in numbers, from the manual’s own tables

All of these come from section 11.0 (Technical specifications) of the Instructions for Use, Rev. E:

  • Maximum user weight: 160 kg. The manual’s weight warning decal says the same.
  • Maximum speed: 10 km/h. Rated slope: 6°.
  • Standard: EN 12184, Class B.
  • Batteries: two 12-volt deep-cycle gel batteries in series (24 V total). The manual lists three fitted sizes: 50 Ah AGM, 60 Ah GEL, or 80 Ah GEL (C20 rating), always as a matched pair.
  • Size: total length 1,070–1,190 mm including legrests, total width 622–660 mm, depending on seating and legrest configuration.
  • Range: 25–40 km depending on battery size and drive-wheel size.

If the chair will not move, the manual’s own troubleshooting list (section 10.0) is six checks before you call anyone: are the batteries charged; switch the chair off and back on; are the battery plugs all securely in place; is the freewheel lever in the DRIVE position; where is the speed regulator set; is the controller locked. Anything beyond that, the manual sends you to the separate controller booklet — the R-net fault codes, translated, are on this site.

What the service documents add, and where they stop

The Service Instructions (000690852.01 Rev. B) are written for technicians, and they are honest about their own limits: “This manual contains the instructions for repairs and general maintenance of the Q400/Q500/Q700 F and R bases.” The M base is not in it. What it does cover, for the F and R bases, is real work: removing the castor wheel from its fork, the fork from the castor arm, the drive wheel from the base, the motor from the base, the battery cover, the brake lever, the brake, and the motor brush — and one procedure that is Q700-only, the removal of the castor damper. It also lists the tools the work needs (flathead and crosshead screwdrivers, ring spanners 13/16/17/19, open-ended spanners 13/22/24, sockets 16/22, hex keys 1.5/2.5/3/4/5/6, a gear puller, and pliers).

The one service document written for the M base itself is the swing-arm installation sheet (248013 Rev. A). It takes you through removing and refitting the rear swing arm — the caster arm — on one side of the Q700M base, and it prints the torque figures: the M10 pivot bolt to 45 ft-lbs (61 Nm), the transit tether to 2.3–2.7 ft-lbs (3.1–3.7 Nm), and the caster locknut to 37 ft-lbs (50 Nm). Its warning is aimed at the person doing the work: “Attention dealers and qualified technicians, do not operate or service this device without first reading the owners manual.”

What is missing, in the open

Frame, drive, seating, and electronics breakdowns for this chair: not published by the maker. That is not a gap in our research — it is the gate, documented. No Illustrated Parts Breakdown or parts catalogue for the Q700 M is published openly by Sunrise; the service instructions that do exist cover the F and R bases and exclude the M; and the separate spare-parts manuals the service instructions point to are not published openly either. The serial number label — Fig. 2.9 in the manual, which shows the label and its contents for each drive layout — is the one key you hold: every part on this chair is orderable by a dealer using it. Until Sunrise publishes the breakdown, a dealer’s parts screen is the only copy — and anything a dealer can see for your chair, you are entitled to ask them to show you.
