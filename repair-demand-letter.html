Demand Letters That Cite the Law

Three fill-in-the-blank letters: a wheelchair owner invoking a state powered-wheelchair repair law, an independent repairer invoking the same, and a rebuttal to a “your warranty is void” threat. Each one names the statute it stands on. Fill the [BRACKETED] fields, attach the evidence listed under the letter, and send it in a form you can prove was sent.

This is education, not legal advice. These templates state what the cited laws say; they are not a lawyer’s review of your facts. If the amount in dispute is large, or anyone threatens you with legal action, get a consumer-protection attorney — several of the laws cited here let a winning consumer recover attorney’s fees, which is exactly the kind of case those attorneys take.

How to use these

  1. Pick the letter that matches who you are — owner, repairer, or warranty holder. Do not blend them; each cites a different legal basis.
  2. Replace every [BRACKET] with your facts. Bracketed choices separated by slashes mean pick one and delete the others. If a field asks for a statute citation, copy it from the matching state entry on The Shield — never cite a law from memory.
  3. Attach what the letter lists. A demand without proof of ownership and the refusal in writing is an opinion. With them, it is a record.
  4. Send it so you can prove sending — email with the thread kept, or paper mail with a certificate of mailing. Keep your copy with the date. Then work the escalation ladder.

Letter 1 — Wheelchair owner invoking a state powered-wheelchair law

Legal basis: your state’s powered-wheelchair statute — Colorado Rev. Stat. §§ 6-1-1501 to 6-1-1505; Cal. Health & Safety Code §§ 21300–21303; Md. Code, Commercial Law §§ 14-5001 to 14-5004; Nev. Rev. Stat. §§ 597.2691 et seq.; Oregon SB 550 (2025) under the Right to Repair Act; R.I. Gen. Laws ch. 6-61; Tenn. Code Ann. §§ 47-18-5701 et seq.; Washington SB 5680 (2025), Title 19 RCW. Full text and limits per state: The Shield, layer two.

[YOUR FULL NAME]
[YOUR ADDRESS]
[YOUR EMAIL] · [YOUR PHONE]

[DATE]

VIA [EMAIL / CERTIFIED MAIL]
[MANUFACTURER LEGAL NAME]
[MANUFACTURER ADDRESS OR SUPPORT EMAIL]

Re: Demand for repair resources under [STATE] law —
[CHAIR MAKE AND MODEL], serial number [SERIAL NUMBER]

Dear [MANUFACTURER]:

I own the powered wheelchair identified above, purchased on
[PURCHASE DATE] from [SELLER]. I am writing to demand the repair
resources that [STATE STATUTE CITATION, e.g., Colo. Rev. Stat.
§ 6-1-1503] requires you to provide to owners of your powered
wheelchairs on fair and reasonable terms:

  - [PART NAME AND PART NUMBER, e.g., "motor/gearbox assembly,
    part no. ______"]
  - [DOCUMENTATION, e.g., "the service manual and fault-code
    table for this model"]
  - [SOFTWARE/TOOL, e.g., "the programming tool and access
    needed to calibrate the replacement part"]

On [DATE OF REFUSAL], your [support line / dealer / email support]
declined to provide these items, stating: "[THE REFUSAL, QUOTED
AS CLOSELY AS YOU CAN]." A copy of that refusal [is attached /
was made verbally by (NAME) on (DATE) and I ask you to confirm
or correct it in writing].

[FOR CALIFORNIA: This letter is also notice of a first violation
under Cal. Health & Safety Code § 21302; I note the statute's
three-day cure provision and ask for compliance within it.]

Please confirm in writing by [DATE — ALLOW AT LEAST 14 DAYS
UNLESS A STATUTE GIVES A SHORTER CURE PERIOD] that the items
above will be made available, the price and terms for each, and
the ordering procedure. If you contend any item is exempt —
for example, as a trade secret, or in California as a part
requiring programming, calibration, or clinical involvement
for seating and positioning — please identify the item and the
specific exemption you rely on.

If I do not have your written confirmation by that date, I will
pursue the remedies the law provides, which [in this state
include a complaint to the Attorney General /
include suit under (CONSUMER PROTECTION ACT CITATION)].

This letter is written in good faith to obtain what the statute
already requires. I would rather have the part than the argument.

[YOUR SIGNATURE]
[YOUR PRINTED NAME]

Attach: proof of ownership (receipt, delivery paperwork, or Medicare/supplier record showing the chair is yours); a photo of the serial-number plate; the refusal in writing if you have it (if the refusal was verbal, say so in the letter, as the template does — and ask them to confirm or correct it in writing); any fault code or diagnosis you already have from the chair itself.

Letter 2 — Independent repair provider invoking the same laws

Legal basis: the same eight state statutes. Every one of them extends the manufacturer’s duty to independent repair providers — a repair business with no authorized-dealer arrangement — on the same fair and reasonable terms. Two practical differences from Letter 1: identify your business precisely, and expect the manufacturer’s trade-secret and (in California) clinical-parts carve-outs to be raised against you first.

[BUSINESS LETTERHEAD — BUSINESS NAME, ADDRESS, PHONE, EMAIL]

[DATE]

VIA [EMAIL / CERTIFIED MAIL]
[MANUFACTURER LEGAL NAME]
[MANUFACTURER ADDRESS OR DEALER-SUPPORT EMAIL]

Re: Independent repair provider demand under [STATE] law —
[CHAIR MAKE AND MODEL], serial number [SERIAL NUMBER]

Dear [MANUFACTURER]:

[BUSINESS NAME] is an independent repair provider located in
[CITY, STATE]. We are not an authorized dealer for your products
and hold no arrangement with you. We are repairing the powered
wheelchair identified above for its owner, [OWNER NAME], with
the owner's authorization [attached].

Under [STATE STATUTE CITATION], you are required to make
available to independent repair providers, on fair and reasonable
terms, the documentation, parts, embedded software, firmware,
and tools used to diagnose, maintain, and repair your powered
wheelchairs. We demand:

  - [PART / DOCUMENTATION / TOOL, WITH PART NUMBERS WHERE KNOWN]
  - [ITEM]
  - [ITEM]

On [DATE], [your parts desk / dealer channel / support line]
[refused this request / quoted terms of (DESCRIBE — e.g., a price
of $___ against a dealer price of $___ ) / stated that these
items are dealer-only]. [A copy of that response is attached.]

Please provide, in writing, by [DATE — AT LEAST 14 DAYS]: the
price and terms for each item above on the same basis you offer
your authorized providers, and the ordering procedure for
independent providers under the statute. If you contend an item
is exempt, identify it and the specific exemption.

Unreasonable delay or dealer-only terms are a violation of
[STATUTE CITATION] [and, in this state, an unfair or deceptive
trade practice under (CONSUMER PROTECTION ACT CITATION)]. We
will document any further refusal for the owner's complaint to
the Attorney General and for our own remedies under the Act.

We repair what you sell, in the open. We would rather do it
with your parts desk than despite it.

[BUSINESS OWNER NAME AND TITLE]
[BUSINESS NAME]

Attach: the owner’s written authorization naming your business and the chair’s serial number; your business license or registration if your state issues one; the refusal or discriminatory quote in writing. Send a copy to the chair’s owner the same day — in several states the owner’s own complaint to the Attorney General carries independent weight, and your file becomes their evidence.

One disclosure note: some state frameworks (California’s and Nevada’s among them) expect an independent provider to tell the customer, in writing, that it is not an authorized repair provider for the product before repairing it. Do it on your invoice if not before; it protects you and costs nothing.

Letter 3 — “Your warranty is void” rebuttal

Legal basis: federal. The Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act, 15 U.S.C. § 2302(c), bars a warrantor from conditioning warranty coverage on the use of branded parts or service unless they are provided free or the FTC has granted a waiver; the FTC’s interpretation at 16 C.F.R. § 700.8 treats void-for-aftermarket-parts terms as deceptive, and its 2022 orders against Harley-Davidson and MWE/Westinghouse required those companies to strike exactly these clauses. If coverage is ultimately refused, 15 U.S.C. § 2310(d) gives you a private right to sue, with attorney’s fees if you prevail. This letter works for any warranted product — chair, console, appliance — because the law is federal.

[YOUR FULL NAME]
[YOUR ADDRESS]
[YOUR EMAIL] · [YOUR PHONE]

[DATE]

VIA [EMAIL / CERTIFIED MAIL]
[WARRANTOR — MANUFACTURER OR SELLER WHO ISSUED THE WARRANTY]
[ADDRESS OR WARRANTY-CLAIMS EMAIL]

Re: Warranty claim [CLAIM/TICKET NUMBER] — unlawful
void-for-repair condition; [PRODUCT, MODEL, SERIAL NUMBER]

Dear [WARRANTOR]:

On [DATE], your [representative / service center / warranty
desk] [denied my warranty claim / stated that my warranty is
void] because [the product was repaired by an independent
provider / a non-branded replacement part was installed /
the warranty seal was broken — DESCRIBE EXACTLY WHAT WAS SAID,
AND BY WHOM].

That condition is unlawful. The Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act,
15 U.S.C. § 2302(c), provides that a warrantor may not condition
warranty coverage on a consumer's use of an article or service
identified by brand, trade, or corporate name, unless the article
or service is provided without charge or the Federal Trade
Commission has waived the prohibition. The FTC's interpretation,
16 C.F.R. § 700.8, is to the same effect, and in 2022 the FTC
ordered Harley-Davidson and MWE Investments (Westinghouse) to
remove identical void-if-repaired terms from their warranties.

If you contend that a specific repair or part in fact caused
the failure now claimed, the burden of demonstrating that is
yours. Please provide, in writing, the specific causal finding
and the evidence for it.

I demand that claim [NUMBER] be reinstated and processed on its
merits, and that you confirm in writing by [DATE — 14 DAYS] that
no void-for-repair condition is being applied to my warranty.
If the claim is refused, 15 U.S.C. § 2310(d) provides a private
action for a warrantor's failure to comply with the Act,
including recovery of attorney's fees, and I will also file
complaints with the FTC and the [STATE] Attorney General.

[YOUR SIGNATURE]
[YOUR PRINTED NAME]

Attach: the warranty document itself (photograph the page with the void-if-repaired clause if that is what they pointed at); proof of purchase and the warranty period; the denial in writing or the ticket transcript; the independent repair invoice, which shows what was actually done — an ordinary repair invoice is usually the strongest answer to a causation claim.

The one thing this letter cannot do: it does not revive a warranty that expired, and it does not make a warrantor pay for damage a bad repair genuinely caused. It removes one specific, unlawful excuse. Everything else about the claim still has to be won on the facts — so keep the facts documented.
