Exploded View: Pride Jazzy 623

The whole Jazzy 623, taken apart by the people who built it. Pride’s Illustrated Parts Breakdown for this chair explodes 16 assemblies into numbered parts. Every diagram below is Pride’s own page, followed by the book’s parts table with a plain-language line added for every part.

Coverage: full IPB. All 16 assemblies and their 199 parts are here. The book’s last two pages are electrical system diagrams — drawings with no parts lists — so they live in the linked book only.

The source

Guide: Pride Mobility Illustrated Parts Breakdown for the Jazzy 623 power chair.

Document: INFMANU4526 Rev. B (the book’s footer code reads Rev.B/022414) — 34 pages, Australian edition.

Full guide (PDF, in this site’s library): pride_jazzy-623_parts.pdf

The book is honest about its own scope, in its notes: “Only serviceable components are shown. Items with lower-case extensions represent right/left specific components.” Callouts marked with an asterisk (*) are parts the book does not illustrate — the book’s introduction says so. The prices printed in the book are Australian dollars; they are not reproduced here — the part numbers are what a parts desk orders by.

The maker’s own warnings

This breakdown prints no warning paragraphs. On Pride’s chairs the safety warnings are decals, and the book lists them as parts — the battery-warning decal (DWR1235L515), the tire-deflation decal (DWR1234L957 — “deflate tire completely” before a wheel comes apart), the EMI/RFI decal (DWR1235L512), and the cord-placement decal (DWR1235L521) are all in the Shroud and Owner’s steps below, with their part numbers.

If a warning decal on your chair is peeling or unreadable, it is a replaceable part. A warning you cannot read cannot warn you.

Build order

The assemblies are laid out in the order you would build the chair: frame first, batteries last. Every step is one exploded view from the maker’s guide, followed by its parts table. The Callout column is the label printed on the diagram. The Part number column is the maker’s own number — the number a parts desk cannot argue with.
