Chair Control — the owner tool

This is BareBoard’s own software, written here, for the person who rides the chair. Plug your phone into your chair, read what it is saying in plain English, see the settings the dealer menu hides, and — where the manufacturer’s own documents prove the path — change them yourself.

New here? Start with the practice chair. It is a make-believe wheelchair inside this page. Every button works. Nothing real can break. Learn the whole tool on it first — that is what it is for.

Version 1. Original software, built from this site’s own published research and the manufacturers’ public manuals. Not a copy of any dealer tool, and not affiliated with any manufacturer.

    How to connect — slowly, in order

    The idea

    Your chair’s parts talk to each other on a private little network inside the chair called its bus — one cable that runs from the joystick down to the power module in the base. This tool joins that conversation. Your phone cannot plug into the chair directly, so one small box sits in between: a CAN adapter. (CAN is the name of the network language cars and chairs both use.)

    Your phone → USB cable → CAN adapter → the chair’s bus cable → your chair

    The one safety fact that matters

    The chair’s bus cable carries 24-volt battery power on the same plug as the data wires. So the adapter must be galvanically isolated — built with an electrical barrier between the chair side and the phone side, rated 2500 volts or better. Isolation is what stops a wiring fault from travelling up the cable into your phone. Only ever connect through an isolated adapter. No exceptions, including “just for a second.” (This is the same rule as Reading the Chair.)

    What to buy (real products, named plainly)

    • CANable (isolated model) — a small open-hardware USB-to-CAN adapter. Buy the isolated version specifically; not every CANable is isolated. It speaks a simple text protocol (called SLCAN) that this page can use directly through the browser — no software to install. Roughly the price of a pizza.
    • PCAN-USB (PEAK) — the professional classic. Excellent hardware, but it uses its maker’s own computer driver, which a web page cannot use. Fine with desktop tools; not for this page in version 1.
    • Bluetooth CAN bridges (various makers) — a wireless adapter that plugs into the chair and talks to the phone by Bluetooth. Each maker uses its own wireless language, so version 1 lists them honestly as not-yet-pairing instead of pretending.
    • The chair end: the adapter’s chair-side wires land on the chair’s round 4-pin bus cable — the cable between the joystick and the power module. Adapter makers sell short pigtail cables for this; the bench method for identifying the connector is on Reading the Chair.

    The steps

    1. Park the chair on level ground. Switch it off. You do not need to be sitting in it — better if you are not.
    2. Plug the adapter into the chair’s bus cable first, while everything is off.
    3. Plug the USB cable from the adapter into your phone (Android) or computer. Chrome browser open on this page.
    4. Pick My real R-net chair above, pick the USB connection, and press Connect. The browser will show a list — choose your adapter.
    5. Switch the chair on. Its messages appear under “What the chair is saying.” You are listening to your own chair.

    What version 1 does on a real chair — honestly

    On a real chair, version 1 listens and reads only: messages, fault codes, and your settings, shown to you. It does not change settings on a real chair yet. Why: this site proved the reading path on captured dealer sessions, but the writing path has not yet been proven on a live power module on the bench — and this site does not guess at writes to a machine a person’s body depends on. The full changing flow — unlock, automatic backup, change, one-tap restore — works today on the practice chair, and moves to real chairs when the bench proof lands. The whole story is on Honest Limits.

    Phones

    Works in Chrome on Android and on computers (Chrome/Edge). An iPhone cannot do this in a browser — Apple does not let web pages use USB accessories or Bluetooth the way this needs. That is a limit of the phone’s system, not a missing feature here.

    Before you touch a real chair

    Before any step that touches a plug, cable, motor, brake, actuator, or battery terminal: put the chair on level ground, switch it OFF, and disconnect the main battery harness or trip the main circuit breaker. Do not work while seated in the chair. Connector pins can remain live after switch-off. At a battery terminal, work on one terminal at a time with an insulated tool. If you cannot disconnect power, inspect visually only and stop. (The same rule heads the fault-code tables.)
