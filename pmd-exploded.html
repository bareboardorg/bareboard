Exploded Views

A power wheelchair is not one expensive assembly. It is a frame, two motors, a handful of wheels, a seat, a controller, and two batteries — and every one of those parts has a number. These pages take each chair apart using the manufacturer’s own parts guide: the exploded diagram the dealer sees, the callout for every component, and the maker’s part number, with one plain line explaining what each part does.

610 manufacturer diagrams and 4605 component rows, across 9 chairs. Where a maker publishes the full breakdown, the page is complete. Where a maker publishes nothing, the page says so, in the open, with the maker’s own words quoted. Nobody should have to guess what their chair is made of.

Full IPB / full catalog means every assembly in the maker’s guide is reproduced, component by component. Partial / manual-only means the maker’s breakdown is not openly available — the page is built from the labeled diagrams in the owner’s manual and says exactly what is missing and why. No diagram on this site is redrawn or invented: if it is not the manufacturer’s own picture, it is not here.

How to use an exploded view

Find your chair’s symptom first, then the assembly that contains it. On the diagram, each part carries a callout — a short label like A1 or a position number. The table under the diagram turns that callout into the maker’s part number and description. That part number is the whole game: it is how you order the $6 bearing instead of the $300 assembly, and it is the number a parts desk cannot argue with.

Assemblies are laid out in build order — frame, drive, motors, wheels, seating, electronics, batteries — so a reader with zero experience can follow how the chair goes together, one step at a time. Safety warnings are the makers’ own, quoted word for word on each page.
