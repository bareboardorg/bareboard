    Quantum Q6 Edge, Opened — Every Part, Named

    This page takes the original Quantum Q6 Edge apart on paper — every assembly, every part, named. It is built from Quantum’s own Illustrated Parts Breakdown, callout by callout, with Quantum’s part numbers and a plain-language line for every part. If your chair is an original Q6 Edge — not an Edge 2.0, 3, or 4 — this is the map of it.

    How to use this page: open the Q6 Edge IPB to the page number given under each table. The IPB’s diagram shows the assembly pulled apart, each part marked with a callout letter and number. Find the callout in the table below it: that row gives you Quantum’s part number, the part’s name, how many the chair takes, and what it does, in one plain line.

    One warning before you start: the electronics changed at serial number JB616611306020: every electronics configuration in this book comes in two versions, and the part numbers differ. Find your serial number — on the chair’s label, usually on the base — before ordering electronics. And this book does not cover the batteries or the charger; those are in the owner’s manual, in the batteries section below.

    About this chair

    The Q6 Edge is Quantum Rehab’s complex-rehabilitation power base — the chair underneath the seating that clinics fit to the rider — sold from about 2007. Its funding codes are K0848, K0849, K0856, K0857, and K0861 (models 3S-SS, 3S-C, 3SP-SS, 3SP-C, and 3MP-SS on the maker’s sell sheet). It is a mid-wheel-drive chair: the big powered wheels sit in the middle, with small swivelling casters front and rear — six wheels on the ground, which is what lets it turn in tight indoor spaces. The motors are 4-pole, rated for 5 or 6 miles per hour, standard or Accu-Trac. It runs on two NF-22 batteries and carries up to 300 lb. TRU-Balance 3 power positioning was available.

    The source for this page is Quantum’s Illustrated Parts Breakdown for the chair, document INFMANU4040 Revision I, dated 27 June 2012, 106 pages. A parts breakdown — an IPB — is the manufacturer’s own list of every serviceable part, with a drawing of each assembly pulled apart and a table of callouts, part numbers, names, and quantities.

    How to read this exploded view

    • Callout — the letter-and-number mark on the IPB’s drawing (A1, B1, J1). Find it on the drawing, find it in the table.
    • Parent assembly — a callout range like A1–A3 means one part number buys the whole assembly: the part plus everything listed under it. The IPB says most parts live inside assemblies and may not be sold separately.
    • No callout (—) — the IPB lists the part as “N/A”: paperwork and labels that have no place on a drawing.
    • An asterisk (*) on a callout — the part is not shown in the diagram. The book says so in the notes under the table.
    • Qty — how many of that part one chair takes.
    • What it is — Quantum’s own name for the part, word for word from the IPB.
    • What it does — our plain-language line, written for this page. It says what the part type does, not which exact parts it joins to — the IPB does not say that, so neither do we.
    • Left and right are from the rider’s point of view, sitting in the chair.
    • Only serviceable components are shown. The book’s own note, printed under most tables: parts Quantum does not sell on their own are not in the drawings.
    • Serial numbers matter. The electronics split at JB616611306020; some front-suspension and freewheel parts split at JB621711001C30. Check your chair’s serial number before ordering.
    • Colors are Quantum’s names (Candy Apple Red, Viper Blue, Sunburst Orange). Every color appears in the shroud table with its own part number.

    4. Drive motors

    Two motors drive this chair, one per side. Each is a motor and gearbox in one unit: the E868 G4 motor geared down — 125 RPM (26.8:1) for the 5 mph chair, 160 RPM (21.3:1) for the 6 mph chair. RPM means revolutions per minute. The Accu-Trac option adds an encoder, a sensor that reports how far the wheel has turned, so the chair drives straighter.

    Batteries and charger

    The IPB does not list the batteries or the charger as parts — this section comes from the owner’s manual and the maker’s sell sheet instead. The Q6 Edge runs on two 12-volt NF-22 deep-cycle batteries — NF-22 is a battery size, not a brand — sealed and maintenance-free, 38 lb each, wired together to make 24 volts. The charger is an 8-amp off-board charger: a separate box that plugs into a wall outlet and into the charger socket on the controller.

    To charge: put the chair next to a standard outlet, switch the controller off, put the chair in drive mode, plug the charger into the controller’s charger socket, then plug it into the wall. When the batteries are full, unplug from the wall first, then from the controller. The charger’s lights show power on, charging, and charged — the charger’s own manual explains them.

    New batteries need breaking in: fully charge them before first use, run the chair gently, then give them another full charge of 8 to 14 hours. After four or five charging cycles they reach full capacity. Always use two batteries of the exact same type, chemistry, and amp-hour capacity — never mix — and always use the supplied off-board charger, never an automotive charger. The charger case can get hot while charging, and the batteries are heavy: 38 lb each.

    What to do with a part number

    1. Write down the callout, the part number, and your chair’s serial number. The serial number is on the chair’s label, usually on the base.
    2. Look the number up on Quantum’s parts lookup, prideprovider.com — the IPB itself points there as the up-to-date lookup — or give it to the dealer and ask them to confirm it against your serial number.
    3. This IPB prints 2012 dealer and list prices next to every part. They are fourteen years old: ask for the current price before you agree to anything, and ask whether the part is sold on its own or only as its parent assembly.
    4. Before you order a battery or a charger, check the chemistry: gel and AGM batteries take different charger settings, and the wrong setting shortens the batteries’ life.

    No 510(k) clearance was found for the original Q6 Edge in the FDA’s database (checked through OpenFDA, 21 November 2026). The 510(k) number K042612 that appears in Pride’s paperwork for other Jazzy chairs names the Jazzy 600, cleared 6 October 2004 — it is not this chair’s clearance, and this page does not claim it as one. The one Q6 Edge 510(k) in the database, K143383, names the Q6 Edge EM — a different chair.

    Adverse-event reports. Five reports in the FDA’s MAUDE database name the plain Q6 Edge (checked through OpenFDA, 21 November 2026). These are reports as filed — allegations, not findings; the FDA database does not say the chair caused what was reported:

    • 2 June 2015, injury (MDR 4834704): a rider’s chair veered uncontrollably to the left and nearly threw her out while going down a ramp.
    • 17 June 2015, malfunction (MDR 5100542): during pressure relief in tilt-in-space, the backrest kept reclining toward the floor; therapists caught the patient before she reached the floor.
    • 15 July 2015, malfunction (MDR 5100572): the backrest upper fell off the chair with a loud noise while it was charging; no injuries; the manufacturer replaced the chair.
    • Received 26 February 2016, malfunction (MDR 5470097): the reporter said the chair was falling apart — the leg-rest pad fell off in front of a counselor, and controller bolts were breaking.
    • 22 June 2016, injury (MDR 6728937): a rider who had switched from another maker’s chair reported problems with the joystick’s position and reach.

    Safety

    Switch the chair OFF and unplug the charger before you touch any part. The batteries stay live even with the chair switched off: never let a metal tool touch two battery terminals at once, and never let a wrench touch a terminal and the frame at the same time.

    The IPB itself prints no prose safety warnings. On this chair the warnings ride as decals — and the IPB lists them as parts in the decal table, including the battery-warnings decal, the deflate-the-tire decal, the EMI-RFI decal, and the extension-cord and grounding-prong caution.     Words we use, defined

    • IPB — Illustrated Parts Breakdown: the manufacturer’s own list of every serviceable part, with a drawing of each assembly pulled apart.
    • Callout — the letter-and-number mark on the drawing that points at one part.
    • Parent assembly — one part number that buys a whole group of parts together (a callout range, like A1–A3).
    • NF-22 — a battery size. Your chair takes two of them.
    • Gel / AGM — the two kinds of sealed lead-acid battery. The charger must be set to match.
    • Q-Logic — Quantum’s controller family, built by Curtis Instruments. Q-Logic 2 is the generation on this chair.
    • NE / NE+ — Quantum’s simpler electronics families, also built on Curtis controllers.
    • Accu-Trac — Quantum’s motor option with an encoder, a sensor that reports how far the wheel has turned, so the chair drives straighter.
    • Mid-Wheel 6 — Quantum’s drive layout: the powered wheels in the middle, with casters front and rear, six wheels on the ground.
    • ATX Suspension — Active-Trac with extra stability: the suspension that lets the front casters ride over bumps while the seat stays level.
    • TRU-Balance — Quantum’s power seating: tilt, recline, and lift driven by electric actuators.
    • Freewheel — the release that lets the chair be pushed by hand when the motors are off.
    • Quantum Ready — an electronics option that lets the chair accept Quantum’s power-seating controls.
