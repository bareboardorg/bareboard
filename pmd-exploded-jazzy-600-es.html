Exploded View: Pride Jazzy 600 ES

Pride does publish an Illustrated Parts Breakdown for the Jazzy 600 ES (INFMANU4643, Rev. H) — but the only public copy we could find sits on a host that refuses every download we attempt. So this page is built from what is openly readable: the labeled figures in the chair’s owner’s manual. The whole chair, the power base, the electrical components, and the assembly view, with every label explained in plain language.

Coverage: partial — owner’s manual figures. No part numbers, because the manual prints none. When a readable copy of the IPB is public, this page gets the full treatment: every assembly, every callout, every part number.

The source

Guide: Owner’s Manual — Jazzy 600 ES
Document: INFMANU4480, Rev. C / June 2014 — Pride Mobility Products Corporation
Full guide (PDF, in this site’s library): pride_jazzy-600-es_owner.pdf

This copy was retrieved from a dealer-posted public copy (Go Mobility, Ireland). The IPB for this chair — INFMANU4643, Rev. H / 032017 — exists; its only located public copy (guidespdf / servls) drops our connections, so it is listed here as a known source gap rather than silently skipped.

The maker’s own warnings, word for word

Pride prints these in the 600 ES owner’s manual, around the freewheel levers and the suspension springs. Word for word:

WARNING! Do not use the power chair while the drive motors are disengaged! Do not disengage the drive motors when the power chair is on an incline, as the unit could roll on its own! Only engage the freewheel mode when on a level surface.

WARNING! It is important to remember that when your power chair is in freewheel mode, the braking system is disengaged.

WARNING! Do not use your foot to operate the manual freewheel levers. Do not stand on the manual freewheel levers. Applying excessive force to the manual freewheel levers may result in damage to the freewheel levers, motors, and brakes.

WARNING! Do not use the freewheel lever handles as tie-down points to secure this product.

PROHIBITED! The spring tension on your power chair was factory set to meet the needs of the demographic majority of users. Do not adjust the tension of any spring on your power chair.

NOTE: Any nylon insert lock nut removed during the disassembly or adjustment of the power chair must be replaced with a new nut. Nylon insert lock nuts should not be reused as it may cause damage to the nylon insert, resulting in a less secure fit. Replacement nylon insert lock nuts are available at local hardware stores or through your authorized Pride Provider.

Verbatim from the Jazzy 600 ES Owner’s Manual, INFMANU4480, Rev. C.

Build order

The assemblies are laid out in the order you would build the chair: frame first, batteries last. Every step is one exploded view from the maker’s guide, followed by its parts table. The Callout column is the label printed on the diagram. The Part number column is the maker’s own number — the number a parts desk cannot argue with.

  • Frame: Step 1: The whole chair (Owner’s Manual Figure 1)
  • Drive: Step 2: The power base (Figure 2)
  • Seating: Step 3: Assembly view — what comes apart (Figure 6)
  • Electronics and controller: Step 4: Electrical components (Figure 3)

Stage 1 — Frame

Start with the skeleton. Every other system bolts to the frame, so it goes together first.

Step 1 — The whole chair (Owner’s Manual Figure 1)

Part (maker’s label)What this part does
SeatbackSupports your back.
Seat assemblyThe whole seat unit: base, back, and armrests together.
ArmrestSupports your arms, and helps you transfer in and out.
JoystickThe hand control you drive with.
ControllerThe chair’s brain: takes the joystick’s commands and drives the motors.
Seat baseThe platform the seat sits on.
Power base assemblyThe bottom half of the chair: frame, motors, wheels, and batteries.
Caster wheelThe small swivel wheels. Not powered &mdash; they follow where the chair turns.
Manual freewheel leverThe lever that disconnects the motors so the chair can be pushed by hand. One on each motor.
Drive wheelThe big powered wheels. The motors turn these.
Spring shock / gas damperSoaks up bumps so the seat rides level.
Front rigging (foot platform)The foot platform your feet rest on.

Stage 2 — Drive

The drive parts carry power from the motors out to the wheels.

Step 2 — The power base (Figure 2)

Part (maker’s label)What this part does
Trapeze barsThe bars at the back of the base that the seat mounts onto.
Rear shroudThe rear body cover. It protects the parts underneath.
Front coverThe front body cover.
Manual freewheel leverDisconnects the motor so the chair can be pushed by hand.
Suspension springSoaks up bumps. Its tension is factory-set — see Pride’s prohibition in the warnings above.

Stage 3 — Seating

Seating goes on once the base rolls. This is the system that holds the rider — fit is safety here.

Step 3 — Assembly view — what comes apart (Figure 6)

Part (maker’s label)What this part does
Armrest angle adjustmentSets the angle of the armrests.
Controller positionWhere the joystick sits &mdash; it can be moved for the rider’s reach.
Seat height/angle adjustmentSets how high the seat sits and how it tilts.
Foot platform angle adjustmentSets the angle of the foot platform.
Foot platform height/depth adjustmentSets where the foot platform sits, up/down and forward/back.

Stage 4 — Electronics and controller

The electronics wire the chair together: controller, joystick, and the cables between them.

Step 4 — Electrical components (Figure 3)

Part (maker’s label)What this part does
Controller power moduleThe box that turns joystick commands into motor power.
Controller connectorWhere the controller’s harness plugs into the power base.
Battery connectorsWhere the batteries plug in. Corrosion here causes many “dead chair” faults.
Motor connectorsWhere each motor plugs into the harness.
Main circuit breakerA resettable safety switch on the rear frame. If the chair dies under load, this may have tripped — let the chair rest a minute, then push it back in.
Quick release battery connectorsBattery plugs you can pull apart by hand — the fast way to make the chair electrically safe.

What is missing, in the open

The full parts breakdown for this chair: published by Pride, but not openly downloadable from anywhere we can reach. If you hold a copy of INFMANU4643, you hold the missing half of this page.
