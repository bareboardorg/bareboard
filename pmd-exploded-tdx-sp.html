Exploded View: Invacare TDX SP

The whole TDX SP, taken apart by the people who built it. Invacare’s Spare Parts Catalogue for this chair — one book covering the TDX-SP, the narrow-base TDX-SP nB, and the TDX Sprint — runs 228 pages. Every plate below is the catalogue’s own page, diagram and table together, followed by the same table with a plain-language line added for every part.

Coverage: full parts catalog. 183 of the catalogue’s plates carry orderable parts — 1,356 rows — and all of them are here. The catalogue’s overview plates list options by name with no part numbers; those are pointers to the detail plates, which are all here.

The source

Guide: Invacare Spare Parts Catalogue for the TDX-SP, TDX-SP nB, and TDX Sprint power wheelchairs.

Document: Version 2015/08/21c EN — 228 pages.

Full guide (PDF, in this site’s library): invacare_tdx-sp_parts.pdf

The catalogue prints a “Valid from → until” date column beside many parts; those dates are not reproduced here — the part numbers are what a parts desk orders by. Rows the catalogue prints without a part number are cross-references to other plates, not parts, and are not counted in the 1,356.

The maker’s own warnings, word for word

A parts catalogue is a book of diagrams, not the chair’s user manual — Invacare’s safety warnings for the TDX SP live in that manual, and the catalogue’s first plates (Stage 7) list the manuals themselves as orderable parts. The one instruction the catalogue prints in its own voice is on the Batteries plate:

“Change batterys only pairwise and from the same type!”

— printed on the Batteries plate of the catalogue itself (position “i”), spelling as printed. Change the pair together, and match the type: the plate lists gel and AGM batteries side by side, and they are not interchangeable.

Build order

The assemblies are laid out in the order you would build the chair: frame first, batteries last. Every step is one exploded view from the maker’s guide, followed by its parts table. The Callout column is the label printed on the diagram. The Part number column is the maker’s own number — the number a parts desk cannot argue with.
