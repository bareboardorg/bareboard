Support BareBoard

Our mission

BareBoard exists so that anyone, anywhere, can learn how the hardware in their life actually works — for free, forever.

The modern device is a locked box. The schematic is a trade secret. The datasheet sits behind a non-disclosure agreement. The battery is glued in, the parts are serialized, the bootloader is signed — and you’re told that opening your own property voids your right to understand it. This is the world as manufactured. We open the box anyway — legally, carefully, and in public.

Every device on this bench gets the full treatment: chip-level identification, boot-log forensics, board mapping, protocol analysis. UART headers, JTAG pads, hidden radios, silent telemetry — if it’s on the board, it goes in the report, written so a curious student can follow along and a seasoned engineer can verify every claim.

This work stands on principles we don’t negotiate:

Learning is a birthright, not a product. Nobody grants you the right to understand the world — you’re born with it. But the knowledge that runs modern life keeps getting fenced off: paywalled manuals, NDA’d datasheets, tuition-gated engineering programs. We publish everything — every trace, every decode, every identification — openly, free, forever, for anyone with curiosity and a connection. No one gets to decide who’s worthy of knowing how things work.

What you bought is yours. Ownership used to mean something. Now it means a license agreement, a subscription for features the hardware already has, and a repair manual you’re not allowed to read. We document devices so owners can actually own them: real board maps, real component IDs, real procedures — the information that repair monopolies depend on you never seeing.

Fixing things is a right. A working device shouldn’t become landfill because a manufacturer moved on. Serialized parts, software-locked components, and “authorized service” gatekeepers aren’t engineering — they’re rent extraction. Documentation is the antidote.

Secrecy serves the seller, not you. The hidden radio broadcasting in your bedroom, the telemetry phoning home at 3 AM, the backdoor nobody documented — if it’s in your house and on your network, you deserve to know it’s there.

The balance of power is wrong, and knowledge corrects it. Right now, corporations decide what your devices do, what they report, and when they die — and you get a terms-of-service checkbox. Every teardown here hands a piece of that power back to the person holding the screwdriver.

No paywall. No ads. No sponsored findings. No selling your data. No investor deciding what gets covered. Just the truth about the circuit boards, published for the people who believe a device you can’t open isn’t really yours.

That’s the mission. Your support keeps the bench powered.

Where every dollar goes

  • Bench devices. Teardowns need hardware — the rarer the device, the more it costs to rescue one from a landfill or an auction listing and get it onto the bench.
  • Test equipment. Logic analyzers, UART adapters, soldering gear, microscopes — the instruments that turn a pile of chips into documented knowledge.
  • Domain & hosting. The unglamorous cost of staying online and staying independent.

A donation buys no influence. Findings are published on the evidence — never on who’s paying. No sponsored teardowns, no embargoed results, no pay-to-play coverage. Ever.

Other ways to help

Money isn’t the only currency here:

  • Spread the word. Send one teardown to one curious person. That does as much as a donation.
  • Donate hardware. Got a dead or retired device gathering dust? It might be our next autopsy. (Contact details on the About page.)
  • Correct us. Found an error in a report? Tell us. Accuracy is the whole product.
  • Send us a target. Know a device with undocumented hardware, a locked bootloader begging for analysis, or a board nobody’s mapped? Point us at it.
  • Link us. Writing about hardware? Cite our findings. Open knowledge grows by being referenced.

We started BareBoard on a simple belief: education should be a human right — free and free to access. Everything here will always be free to read. No paywall, no premium tier, no exceptions. Thank you for keeping it that way.

Allies & further reading

We’re one bench in a much bigger movement. These are the organizations and communities fighting the same fight:

  • Open Source Hardware Association (OSHWA) — the home of the open-hardware definition, community certification, and the annual Open Hardware Summit.
  • Electronic Frontier Foundation — Right to Repair — legal advocacy against DRM, software locks, and anti-circumvention abuse.
  • iFixit — the repair community’s workshop: free teardowns, repair guides, and parts for thousands of devices.
  • Repair.org / The Repair Association — the coalition pushing right-to-repair legislation across the US.
  • Free Software Foundation — defending users’ freedom to run, study, share, and modify software since 1985.
  • Open Source Initiative — stewards of the Open Source Definition and license review.
  • Creative Commons — the licensing infrastructure that makes sharing knowledge legally simple.
  • What is Free Software? — the GNU Project’s foundational essay on user freedom. Required reading.

BareBoard Legal Framework & Declarations

  • No AI training: All text, findings, and original photography on this site are copyrighted. Automated scraping for AI model training is prohibited. Crawlers that ignore robots.txt are trespassing.
  • Educational nature: BareBoard operates exclusively as a non-commercial, open educational resource. All material is compiled for academic evaluation, hardware preservation, and technical literacy under 17 U.S.C. § 107 (fair use).
  • Zero-leak / no-binary clause: This site does not host, distribute, or mirror proprietary firmware binaries, encrypted operating system keys, or weaponizable exploits. Documentation covers only the public, physical realities of circuit hardware.
  • Property verification: Every hardware autopsy is performed on personally owned, legally acquired equipment. No rented or third-party infrastructure is used.
  • Public source sourcing: Comparative and regulatory data is aggregated from official public filings, including the U.S. Federal Communications Commission equipment authorization database.
