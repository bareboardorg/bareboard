Exploded View: Pride Jazzy EVO 613

This page is different, and the difference is the story. Pride Mobility does not publish an Illustrated Parts Breakdown for the Jazzy EVO 613 — not for owners, not openly at all. What Pride does publish is the labeled overview in the owner’s manual — the whole chair, the power base, and the electrical parts, each with its own diagram — plus an order form that names real part numbers for the batteries, joysticks, seats, armrests, and leg rests. This page puts both together, and names the gap exactly: everything below the order form — motors, gearboxes, casters, shrouds, harnesses — has no public part number, because Pride sells those parts through its dealers.

Coverage: manual-only, plus order-form part numbers. The Jazzy EVO 613 is a mid-wheel-drive power chair: two in-line motors, Active-Trac suspension, a 40-amp PG GC3 controller, and a choice of two batteries — two sealed lead-acid U-1 batteries, or, as the EVO 613 Li, one 25-volt lithium iron phosphate pack. The Li version is the same chair with a different battery and joystick: 160.5 lb instead of 189 lb, and up to 21 miles per charge instead of 17.1. Weight capacity is 300 lb. The chair is 22 inches wide and turns in a 22-inch radius, which is why it fits through tight indoor corners.

The maker’s own warnings, word for word

Pride prints these in the EVO 613 owner’s manual. Word for word:

An authorized Pride Provider or a qualified technician must perform the initial setup of this power chair and must perform all of the procedures in this manual.

Never operate your power chair if it is behaving abnormally or erratically.

Do not use the power chair while the drive motors are disengaged! Do not disengage the drive motors when the power chair is on an incline or decline, as the unit could roll on its own. Only engage the freewheel mode when on a level surface.

It is important to remember that when your power chair is in freewheel mode, the braking system is disengaged.

The power elevating seat option is intended for use on a level surface only. Never raise the seat from its lowest position on an inclined surface. Failure to heed this warning can result in the power chair tipping over.

The batteries in your power chair should only be serviced or replaced by an authorized Pride Provider or a qualified technician.

Explosive gases may be generated while charging the battery. Keep the power chair and battery charger away from sources of ignition such as flames or sparks and provide adequate ventilation when charging the battery.

Verbatim from the Pride Jazzy EVO 613 Owner’s Manual, INFMANU5239 Rev. B, sections II, III, VI, and VII.

Start with the two halves. The chair has two main assemblies: the seat assembly (A) and the power base assembly (B). The seat assembly is the armrests, seatback, and seat base. The power base assembly is two motor/brake assemblies, two drive wheels, the batteries, and the wiring harnesses.

Stage 2 — Electronics

The electrical parts live on or inside the power base. The main circuit breaker is on the front of the base; the power module sits under the front cover. The power module is the box that connects the joystick to the motors, the batteries, and the off-board charger. If the circuit breaker trips — it trips when the batteries and motors are heavily strained — let the chair rest for about a minute, push the breaker button back in, turn the controller on, and carry on. If it keeps tripping, call your Pride Provider.

Stage 3 — Freewheel levers

Each motor has a manual freewheel lever. The levers let you disconnect the drive motors from the gearboxes so the chair can be pushed by hand. Pull both levers up to engage the motors (drive mode); push both down to disengage them (freewheel mode). If a lever is hard to move, rock the chair gently back and forth and try again. Never use your foot on the levers, never stand on them, and never use the lever handles as tie-down points. And remember the warning above: in freewheel mode the brakes are off, so only ever do this on level ground with someone there to hold the chair.

Stage 4 — Seat and controller assembly

The seat slides down onto the power base, and the controller fits into one of the armrests and is tightened with the supplied hex key — a small L-shaped wrench. The controller cable is the fragile part of this job: lift the armrest straight up first, route the cable under the armrest or toward the inside of the armrest pad, never on the outside, and tie it down at the right points so it cannot catch in the wheels, get pinched in the seat frame, or snag in a doorway. Then plug it into the connector on the power base. Never pick the seat frame up by the armrests — they pivot freely and you can lose control of the seat.

Stage 5 — Batteries

Two battery choices, two different installations. The standard EVO 613 carries two 12-volt U-1 sealed lead-acid batteries; the EVO 613 Li carries one 25-volt lithium iron phosphate battery. U-1 is a battery size standard — the size, not a brand. AGM (absorbed glass mat) and gel cell are the two sealed lead-acid types Pride accepts; both are sealed and maintenance-free. LiFePO4 (lithium iron phosphate) is the lithium chemistry in the Li pack.

To change a battery: turn the controller off, make sure the chair is in drive mode, remove the top shroud, undo the thumbscrews and remove the rear shroud, and disconnect the battery harness before lifting the battery out. The wiring rule is printed on the chair and in the manual: red wire to the red positive (+) terminal, black wire to the black negative (−) terminal. SLA batteries go in with their terminals facing the front of the base; the lithium battery goes in with its terminals facing the rear. Never mix an old battery with a new one — replace both SLA batteries at the same time. A U-1 battery weighs 23.5 lb; the lithium pack weighs 18.5 lb. Get help lifting them.

Charging: use only the supplied 3.5-amp off-board charger — never a car battery charger. Charge 8 to 14 hours after daily use, plus 4 more hours after the charger says it is done, and give the batteries one continuous 48-hour charge once a month. The charger will not work at all once the batteries have dropped below 18 volts; if that happens, call your Pride Provider. The chair also has a charge reminder: if you drive for 8 hours or more, switch off, and do not charge, the left red light on the battery meter will flash 4 times in half-second bursts every time you switch off, until you complete one full charge with the controller switched on throughout.

The full record

Recalls. No FDA recall names the Jazzy EVO 613. The recalls filed under the same 510(k) number, K042612, cover other models: the Jazzy Select 6 with XLR port harness (2010) and the Quantum Q6 Edge 2.0 and Q6 Edge HD software (posted 31 July 2026, Z-2845-2026 and Z-2846-2026). Neither touches this chair.

Clearance. The FDA’s 510(k) database entry for K042612 names the device JAZZY 600, cleared 6 October 2004 — that is the Jazzy 600, not this chair. Pride’s own adverse-event reports for the EVO 613 cite K042612 as the chair’s 510(k) number, and Pride sells the EVO 613 as an FDA Class II medical device. The public record does not show a separate EVO 613 clearance, so this page does not claim one. The 510(k) summary is in the library: K042612.pdf. The database entry is at FDA accessdata, K042612.

Adverse-event report. One report is on file with the FDA for a Jazzy EVO 613 (MDR Report Key 20358998, report 2530130-2024-00598). A consumer alleged that on 25 September 2024 the chair hit a blind spot, ran into a curb, and flipped onto him, injuring ligaments; the chair was manufactured 1 September 2023 and was not evaluated by the manufacturer. An adverse-event report is an allegation, not a finding — it is included here because the full record includes it. FDA MAUDE report 20358998.

What is missing, in the open

Motor and gearbox breakdowns, caster and wheel breakdowns, shroud and frame breakdowns, and harness part numbers for this chair: not published by the maker. That is not a gap in our research — it is the gate, documented. Pride distributes parts through its dealers, and the order form is the only public document that names part numbers. The serial number label on your chair is the one key you hold: every part on this chair is orderable by a dealer using it. Until Pride publishes the breakdown, a dealer’s parts screen is the only copy — and anything a dealer can see for your chair, you are entitled to ask them to show you.
