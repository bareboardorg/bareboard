    iPad

    The big iPhone board that isn’t a phone: an A-series or M-series computer where the screen is the structure, the battery is two cells, and — since May 2025 — the first Apple tablet line with a genuine public parts book.

    FCC ID

    FCC IDs vary by model and generation. Apple’s grantee code is BCG. Every iPad files separately for each radio configuration — Wi-Fi-only models file once; cellular models file again for their LTE/5G radios — and the filings include the internal photos that are the starting point for reading any iPad board.

    Every radio a device carries — Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular, ultra-wideband, NFC — needs an FCC equipment authorization before the device can be sold in the United States. To get one, the manufacturer files photographs and test reports with the FCC, and those filings are public record. Check the label on your specific unit and look it up at fccid.io.

    What the FCC filing revealed

    The FCC filing for this device includes the documents manufacturers are required to submit: external photos, internal photos, test setup photos, RF test reports, and the user manual. These are public record at fccid.io.

    • Internal photos — the logic board’s shape (a narrow strip along one edge on most models), the two battery cells, antenna lines in the chassis, and the speaker chambers
    • Block diagrams — for the cellular models, the RF chain from modem to antenna
    • Test reports — SAR (specific absorption rate) data for cellular models, which tells you where the radios sit and how hard they transmit

    What the FCC filing does not include: schematics (withheld under confidentiality requests where the maker files them), firmware, and the full bill of materials.

    Exploded view — the assemblies in build order

    This is the centerpiece of the page: the device taken apart into its assemblies, in the order they go together, every part named in plain language with what it does. An exploded view is the drawing a manufacturer makes showing every part separated along the path it assembles — the single most useful document in repair, and the one most often kept behind a dealer login.

    The source

    Parts book: Apple’s Self Service Repair program publishes genuine repair manuals with exploded diagrams and parts lists — but only for iPad Air (M2 and later), iPad Pro (M4), iPad mini (A17 Pro), and iPad (A16), from May 29, 2025. Older models: no public Apple parts book; the exploded view below is rebuilt from the FCC filings’ internal photos, which show the same assembly stack. Board numbers: iPad logic boards carry Apple’s 820- number system (a board marked 820-xxxxx is identified by that number across the repair trade) — the same system the site documents for Mac boards in Level 1.

    Coverage: PARTIAL — full public parts book for the 2025 Self Service Repair models only; FCC internal-photo basis for every earlier generation. No diagram on this page is fabricated: where Apple publishes nothing, the table names the assemblies the FCC photos show, in build order.

    Build order

    Step 1 — Chassis & antennas

    The aluminum unibody shell is the frame and the ground plane. Everything bolts or glues into it.

    PartWhat this part does
    Unibody enclosureThe structural shell. Its machined pockets set where every other assembly sits.
    Antenna lines & windowsBreaks in the metal where the Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and (on cellular models) LTE/5G antennas radiate. The plastic-filled seams on the back are not decoration.
    Speaker chambersTwo or four sealed cavities at the edges. They are acoustic boxes, not empty space — crushing one changes the sound.

    Step 2 — Logic board

    One narrow board along one edge carries the whole computer. On a phone this board is stacked and folded; the iPad has room to lay it flat, which is why the iPad board is the better teaching board.

    PartWhat this part does
    A-series / M-series SoCThe system on a chip: CPU, GPU, and memory controller in one package. iPad used phone-class A chips, then crossed to Mac-class M chips (M1, 2021; M4, 2024).
    NAND flash storageThe soldered storage chip beside the SoC. Not removable, not upgradeable — the capacity you buy is the capacity there is.
    Power management IC (PMIC)Turns battery voltage into the dozen-plus rails the SoC needs. Identified in this site’s library: Apple PMIC Identification Guide.
    Secure EnclaveThe separate security processor inside the SoC that holds fingerprint/face templates and keys. Public reference in this site’s library: Apple Secure Enclave — Public Reference.
    Wi-Fi / Bluetooth radioA combo radio chip (Broadcom-designed in most generations) feeding the antenna lines.
    Cellular modem (cellular models)A Qualcomm modem on cellular models, with its own RF front end — the second radio system in the filing.

    Step 3 — Battery — two cells

    The iPad battery is two flat lithium cells side by side, joined as one pack. Two cells spread the weight, fit the thin shell, and share the load — and they age together, so a swollen or weak pack is replaced as a unit.

    PartWhat this part does
    Battery cells (2)The energy store. Glued to the chassis with adhesive strips; the pull-tabs are the designed removal path where fitted.
    Battery connector & boardThe pack’s protection board and the connector to the logic board — the first thing disconnected in any repair, the last reconnected.

    Step 4 — Cameras, sensors & buttons

    Small modules on flex cables around the frame’s edge.

    PartWhat this part does
    Front camera & Face ID / Touch IDFace ID models carry a projector + camera pair in the bezel; Touch ID models put the fingerprint sensor in the top button.
    Rear cameraA single camera (two on some Pro models, plus a LiDAR scanner that measures depth with light pulses).
    Buttons & flex cablesPower and volume switches on thin flex — the part most often torn during a screen opening.
    Smart Connector / magnetic Pencil contactsThe pogo-pin or magnetic interface that powers accessories without a port.

    Step 5 — Display assembly — on last, off first

    The screen is the lid and the structure’s front face. It is laminated (glass, touch layer, and LCD/OLED fused) on most models, which is why a cracked digitizer usually means a whole display assembly.

    PartWhat this part does
    Cover glass & digitizerThe touch-sensing layer. On non-laminated models (base iPad line) it separates from the LCD and can be replaced alone — the cheapest screen repair in the family.
    LCD / OLED panelThe picture itself. Pro models (M4) use a tandem OLED stack — two OLED layers for brightness.
    Display flex cablesThe ribbons that carry video and touch to the logic board, and the first casualty of a careless pry.

    What is missing, in the open: board-level schematics and boardview files for every iPad are Apple-confidential. The repair trade works from the 820- board number, the FCC photos, and the Self Service Repair manuals. Anyone selling you a “leaked full schematic pack” is selling you a document this page will not link, because hosting it would break the rule this site runs on: public records and published documents only.

    Models & chips

    Fifteen years of iPads reduce to one pattern: a phone chip in a bigger shell, until the Pro line crossed over to Mac chips.

    Model lineYearsChip classNotes

    iPad Air line2013–A7 → M-class from Air 5Air (M2 and later) is in Self Service Repair.
    iPad mini line2012–A5 → A17 Promini (A17 Pro) is in Self Service Repair.
    iPad Pro line2015–A9X → M4Pro (M4) is in Self Service Repair; Pro models add LiDAR and ProMotion displays.

    Key chips & components

    • Apple A/M-series SoCs — no public datasheet exists for any of them; Apple publishes nothing at chip level. What is public: the FCC filings, the repair manuals, and this site’s identification references — Apple PMIC Identification Guide and Apple Secure Enclave — Public Reference (site library).
    • Qualcomm cellular modems (cellular models) — the modem family varies by generation; Qualcomm publishes product briefs, not full datasheets. Datasheet: not in the site index yet — logged as a collection target.
    • Broadcom Wi-Fi/Bluetooth combo radios — identified from FCC internal photos per model; full datasheets are under NDA. Collection target logged.

    Faults — which part fails, and how you know

    The exploded view shows you what the parts are. This list tells you which one has failed — symptom first, the way a bench diagnosis actually runs.

    • Cracked glass, touch still works — on base-line (non-laminated) models, the digitizer alone. On laminated models, plan on the full display assembly.
    • Won’t charge, or charges only at one cable angle — the charging port flex (a replaceable part on Self Service Repair models: displays, batteries, cameras, and external charging ports are the published part set).
    • Fast drain or a lifting screen — a swelling battery pack. Stop using it on charge, and treat the pack as the fault, not the screen it is pushing up.
    • Dead after a drop, no crack — a torn display or button flex, or a board-level fracture at the SoC. Flex first; board last.
    • Face ID / Touch ID dead after a screen swap — pairing. Biometric parts are cryptographically tied to the SoC; Apple’s post-repair calibration (System Configuration, run through Self Service Repair) is the documented path that restores them.

    Safety, before you open anything

    The battery is the hazard in this device. Two lithium cells, glued in, thin enough to puncture with a pry tool. Work discharged (below 25% where you can), never lever against a cell, and if a cell bends, creases, or smells sweet/solvent-like, stop — that pack is done and goes to a battery recycler, not the bin.

    The display is the second hazard: it is the structural lid, it is glued, and it cracks into sharp laminated shards when pried cold. Apple’s own manuals specify heated opening and suction fixtures for a reason. This page teaches you what is inside; the manual for your exact model teaches the opening procedure. Read it first.

    Did you know

    • The iPad battery is two separate cells — every iPad ever made spreads its pack across the chassis instead of using one big cell.
    • The thinnest Apple product is a tablet: the iPad Pro (M4) is 5.1 mm — thinner than the iPod nano.
    • For its first fifteen years, the iPad had no public parts book at all — the 2025 Self Service Repair manuals are the first, and they cover only four model families.
