Documentation Archive

Manufacturers publish documentation at product launch and withdraw it when parts go end-of-life. That is precisely when the documentation is most needed. BareBoard maintains a permanent, community-owned archive of component documentation so that repair, education, and research are never gated by a manufacturer's product lifecycle.

Browse the archive: BareBoard Manufacturer Documentation Archive at the Internet Archive — every file free to download, no account required.

Collection Scope

Documentation is collected and preserved in the following priority order:

Current Collection

  • Component datasheets. Every revision, not only the latest. Prior revisions document what changed between silicon or specification updates.
  • Device and board schematics. Board revision recorded in the filename. An unversioned schematic is a liability.
  • Evaluation board packages. Schematic, bill of materials, Gerber files, user manual, and test procedure preserved as a set.

High-Priority Preservation

  • End-of-life notices and last-time-buy announcements. Manufacturers withdraw these from public view. Once removed, there may be no remaining evidence that a component existed in a given revision.
  • Errata sheets. Documented silicon and specification corrections that are later withdrawn from distribution.
  • Product change notifications. Die revisions, fabrication transfers, and specification changes issued during a product's active life.

Reference Depth

  • Application notes. Manufacturer-published implementation guidance, including reference circuits, layout recommendations, and calculation methods.
  • Reference designs. Manufacturer-validated application circuits, frequently including schematics.
  • Reference manuals. Full technical documentation for microcontrollers and systems-on-chip.
  • Package drawings and land patterns. Mechanical dimensions required for board design and rework.
  • Simulation models. SPICE and IBIS models for circuit and signal-integrity analysis.

Rare and At-Risk Material

  • Out-of-print databooks. Manufacturer databooks from prior decades, digitized and preserved.
  • Discontinued distributor and manufacturer archives. Documentation from companies that have ceased operation or been acquired.
  • Regulatory filings. Public equipment authorization records, including internal photographs and schematics submitted as part of the public record.
  • Published periodical schematics. Circuits published in electronics periodicals.

Organization

The archive is organized by manufacturer. Within each manufacturer, documentation is separated by type: datasheets, schematics, and supporting material. Subdivision by product family is introduced only when a manufacturer's collection warrants it. File naming follows the manufacturer's own part numbering so that documents sort and identify correctly.

The archive copy serves as a preservation mirror. Manufacturer-original sources remain the primary reference while available. When a manufacturer link ceases to resolve, the archived copy becomes the primary reference.

Publication Policy

BareBoard publishes documentation in service of the right to repair. The archive contains only documentation that is publicly available or lawfully community-supplied — manufacturer publications, public filings, and openly licensed materials. BareBoard does not collect leaked internal documents, stolen files, or NDA material. The archive exists so that the ability to understand, maintain, and repair hardware is not contingent on a manufacturer’s continued commercial interest in the component.

BareBoard Legal Framework & Declarations

  • No AI training: All text, findings, and original photography on this site are copyrighted. Automated scraping for AI model training is prohibited. Crawlers that ignore robots.txt are trespassing.
  • Educational nature: BareBoard operates exclusively as a non-commercial, open educational resource. All material is compiled for academic evaluation, hardware preservation, and technical literacy under 17 U.S.C. § 107 (fair use).
  • Zero-leak / no-binary clause: This site does not host, distribute, or mirror proprietary firmware binaries, encrypted operating system keys, or weaponizable exploits. Documentation covers only the public, physical realities of circuit hardware.
  • Property verification: Every hardware autopsy is performed on personally owned, legally acquired equipment. No rented or third-party infrastructure is used.
  • Public source sourcing: Comparative and regulatory data is aggregated from official public filings, including the U.S. Federal Communications Commission equipment authorization database.

BareBoard — open hardware allies, documented in alignment with the Open Source Hardware Association framework. Text and original photography published under open terms; nothing here is for sale.
