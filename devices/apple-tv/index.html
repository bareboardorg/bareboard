    Apple TV

    A fanless A-series box with no user-serviceable anything inside — and the quietest record of any Apple line. The board tour comes from the FCC filing, because Apple has never published anything deeper.

    FCC ID

    FCC IDs vary by model. Apple’s grantee code is BCG. Every Apple TV files for Wi-Fi and Bluetooth (the remote pairs over Bluetooth; newer remotes add no radio of their own beyond it), and the internal photos show a board that is mostly power supply and heatsink.

    Every radio a device carries — Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular, ultra-wideband, NFC — needs an FCC equipment authorization before the device can be sold in the United States. To get one, the manufacturer files photographs and test reports with the FCC, and those filings are public record. Check the label on your specific unit and look it up at fccid.io.

    What the FCC filing revealed

    The FCC filing for this device includes the documents manufacturers are required to submit: external photos, internal photos, test setup photos, RF test reports, and the user manual. These are public record at fccid.io.

    • Internal photos — the single board: SoC under a large heatsink, the integrated power supply section, and the antenna placements at the case edges
    • Test reports — the Wi-Fi radio’s output per band, which changed when the 2022 model made Ethernet and Thread model-dependent
    • The user manual — filed publicly; it documents the ports and the remote pairing the filings cover

    What the FCC filing does not include: schematics (withheld under confidentiality requests where the maker files them), firmware, and the full bill of materials.

    Exploded view — the assemblies in build order

    This is the centerpiece of the page: the device taken apart into its assemblies, in the order they go together, every part named in plain language with what it does. An exploded view is the drawing a manufacturer makes showing every part separated along the path it assembles — the single most useful document in repair, and the one most often kept behind a dealer login.

    The source

    Parts book: none. No Self Service Repair coverage, no published manual, no parts. FCC internal photos are the source for the stack below. The unit is held together with clips and screws under the base — it can be opened (unlike the Watch and AirPods, it is not welded), but Apple sells no part you could install, so the exploded view is a diagnostic map, not a shopping list.

    Coverage: PARTIAL — no public parts book; FCC internal-photo basis.

    Build order

    Step 1 — Case & power supply

    The box is a mains-powered device: the power supply is inside, which is why the cord has no wall wart.

    PartWhat this part does
    Case & baseA plastic shell on a rubberized base. The base is the way in.
    Integrated power supplyA small AC-DC supply section on the main board. This is the part that ages: its capacitors are the documented weak point of fanless always-on boxes.
    HeatsinkA large metal mass over the SoC. Fanless means the case and this sink are the cooling system.

    Step 2 — Logic board

    One board, phone-class silicon, TV outputs.

    PartWhat this part does
    A-series SoCA8 (HD) through A15 Bionic (4K, 3rd gen). The same chip family as the iPad, clocked for a box with no battery limit.
    RAM & NAND storageSoldered. 32–128 GB storage tiers are board variants, not upgrades.
    Ethernet controller (equipped models)Present on the 128 GB 4K (3rd gen); the 64 GB model omits Ethernet and Thread — check which board you have before blaming the network.
    HDMI & radiosHDMI output with CEC (the protocol that lets the TV remote and the Apple TV wake each other); Wi-Fi/Bluetooth combo radio; Thread radio on equipped models.

    Step 3 — The Siri Remote — a separate device

    The remote is a Bluetooth peripheral with its own battery and its own failure modes.

    PartWhat this part does
    Remote batteryRechargeable, internal, and not sold as a part. A remote that dies in a day is a service/replacement item.
    Touch surface / clickpadThe 1st-generation Siri Remote’s all-touch face was redesigned in 2021 to a clickpad with a touch ring after sustained user criticism.
    USB-C / Lightning portCharging only. No data path for owners.

    What is missing, in the open: schematics and parts, as with every Apple TV. But note what the FCC photos prove: this is a simple, well-made board whose most likely death (power-supply capacitors) is a fault any bench could fix — if the parts and documentation were sold. They are not.

    What we reveal

    The FCC tells you what a device emits. We tell you what’s inside and how it works.

    • The model split that matters — Ethernet and Thread exist on one 2022 variant and not the other; the box does not tell you from the front
    • Why these die — always-on, fanless, mains-powered: the PSU ages on wall-clock time, not usage time

    Models

    Three hardware eras: the original hobbyist box, the HD, and the 4K line.

    ModelYearsChipNotes
    Apple TV HD (4th gen)2015A8First tvOS model; App Store arrives.
    Apple TV 4K (1st gen)2017A10XAdds 4K/HDR.
    Apple TV 4K (2nd gen)2021A12Ships with the redesigned clickpad remote.
    Apple TV 4K (3rd gen)2022A1564 GB model drops Ethernet + Thread; 128 GB keeps both.

    Key chips & components

    • Apple A8–A15 SoCs — phone silicon in a mains box; no public datasheets. See the Apple PMIC Identification Guide (site library) for the power parts around them.

    Faults — which part fails, and how you know

    A box with no moving parts fails in a short list of ways.

    • Completely dead, no light — the power supply section. On a fanless always-on device this is the expected end-of-life fault; check the cord and outlet first, then accept it is board-level.
    • Reboot loops under load — heat and a tired PSU: the heatsink path is passive, so dust and a closed cabinet are real causes. Ventilate before condemning it.
    • Remote eats its battery — the remote’s cell, or a stuck touch surface keeping it awake. The remote is the highest-failure part of the system and the cheapest to replace.
    • No network on a wired setup — confirm the model: the 64 GB 4K (3rd gen) has no Ethernet port at all. Owners have “repaired” a port their model never had.

    Safety, before you open anything

    The power supply is inside this box and connects to mains. Its capacitors can hold a charge after unplugging. If you open one, treat the PSU section the way you would any mains supply: no probing a live board, and no hurry around the big capacitors.

    An honest record section for this device is short — and the page says so rather than inflating it.

    Did you know

    • There is no fan and never has been: every Apple TV cools a phone chip with a lump of metal and patience.
    • The box has no power brick because the power supply is on the board — which is also why it is the part that eventually kills the box.
