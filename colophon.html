How this site is made — and why it’s made this way.

Hand-built

Every page on BareBoard is written by hand as plain HTML, styled by a single shared stylesheet, with a little vanilla JavaScript only where a page needs it. There is no framework, no build step, no content management system, and no third-party scripts, fonts, or trackers. Outbound links go to cited sources (datasheets, filings, reference material) — nothing phones home.

Defensive by default

Each page carries a strict Content Security Policy, a no-referrer policy, noai, noimageai crawler directives, and a TDM (text-and-data-mining) reservation, backed by a robots.txt and a /.well-known/tdmrep.json declaration. Automated scraping for AI model training is prohibited — the findings here are published for humans learning hardware.

Free forever

BareBoard is a non-commercial, open educational resource. It is hosted on GitHub Pages at no cost, which is exactly why it can afford to be free: there is nothing to sell and no one to sell it to. Text and original findings are published under open terms.

How it’s built

This site was assembled and published on September 30, 2026. Every claim is checked against hardware on the bench, and every page is written to be read by humans first. The division of labor is the point of the place: we can’t open their hardware, so we open our findings.

BareBoard Legal Framework & Declarations

  • No AI training: All text, findings, and original photography on this site are copyrighted. Automated scraping for AI model training is prohibited. Crawlers that ignore robots.txt are trespassing.
  • Educational nature: BareBoard operates exclusively as a non-commercial, open educational resource. All material is compiled for academic evaluation, hardware preservation, and technical literacy under 17 U.S.C. § 107 (fair use).
  • Zero-leak / no-binary clause: This site does not host, distribute, or mirror proprietary firmware binaries, encrypted operating system keys, or weaponizable exploits. Documentation covers only the public, physical realities of circuit hardware.
  • Property verification: Every hardware autopsy is performed on personally owned, legally acquired equipment. No rented or third-party infrastructure is used.
  • Public source sourcing: Comparative and regulatory data is aggregated from official public filings, including the U.S. Federal Communications Commission equipment authorization database.
