    Apple Watch

    The smallest sealed computer Apple sells. There is no board to probe: the whole computer is one resin-potted module, and the FCC filing’s photographs are the closest thing to a teardown the public record allows.

    FCC ID

    FCC IDs vary by model and case size. Apple’s grantee code is BCG. GPS + Cellular models file for their LTE and ultra-wideband radios as well as Wi-Fi/Bluetooth — and because the device is glued shut and never opened by its owner, the internal photos in those filings are the only official look inside that exists.

    Every radio a device carries — Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular, ultra-wideband, NFC — needs an FCC equipment authorization before the device can be sold in the United States. To get one, the manufacturer files photographs and test reports with the FCC, and those filings are public record. Check the label on your specific unit and look it up at fccid.io.

    What the FCC filing revealed

    The FCC filing for this device includes the documents manufacturers are required to submit: external photos, internal photos, test setup photos, RF test reports, and the user manual. These are public record at fccid.io.

    • Internal photos — the S-series module as a single potted unit, the Taptic Engine’s share of the case, the battery, and the antenna layout in the case and band lugs
    • Test reports — SAR data measured at the wrist, which bounds how hard the cellular radio may transmit
    • The user manual — filed with the FCC; it documents the magnetic charging puck interface and the sensor array on the case back

    What the FCC filing does not include: schematics (withheld under confidentiality requests where the maker files them), firmware, and the full bill of materials.

    Exploded view — the assemblies in build order

    This is the centerpiece of the page: the device taken apart into its assemblies, in the order they go together, every part named in plain language with what it does. An exploded view is the drawing a manufacturer makes showing every part separated along the path it assembles — the single most useful document in repair, and the one most often kept behind a dealer login.

    The source

    Parts book: none. Apple publishes no parts book, no service manual, and no Self Service Repair path for the Watch. FCC internal photos are the primary source: they show the assembly stack below — case, potted computer module, battery, Taptic Engine, display — and they are what this exploded view is rebuilt from. Apple’s own service model is whole-unit or module replacement through Apple; there is no published procedure at any level.

    Coverage: PARTIAL — no public parts book. FCC internal-photo basis. The stack below is real; the part numbers don’t exist publicly, because Apple has never published any.

    Build order

    Step 1 — Case & antennas

    The case is the chassis, the antenna ground, and the waterproofing. Series models are rated for swimming; the Ultra line adds a depth gauge and water-temperature sensor.

    PartWhat this part does
    Case (aluminum / titanium / steel)The frame. Antennas are built into the case and the display’s edge — there is no antenna part to replace.
    Digital Crown & side buttonThe rotary input (with an optical encoder) and the button; on ECG models the crown is also an electrode.
    Speaker & microphone portsTiny acoustic ports with membranes. The speaker doubles as the water-ejection pump you hear after a swim (it plays a tone to push water out).

    Step 2 — The computer — one potted module

    The S-series SiP (System in Package) is the entire computer — processor, memory, storage, radios, and power management — sealed in resin as a single component. A SiP is a whole circuit board shrunk and potted into one part. It cannot be opened, probed, or repaired; it can only be replaced, and Apple does not sell it.

    PartWhat this part does
    S-series SiPCPU, GPU, RAM, flash, Wi-Fi/Bluetooth (and LTE/UWB on cellular models), and the Secure Enclave in one sealed block.
    Sensor array (case back)The optical heart-rate sensor (green/infrared LEDs + photodiodes), blood-oxygen LEDs on equipped models, and the second electrode for ECG. It is part of the case-back crystal assembly.

    Step 3 — Battery & Taptic Engine

    The two biggest occupants after the module: a small lithium cell and the linear actuator that taps your wrist.

    PartWhat this part does
    Battery cellA single small lithium-ion cell, glued in. Apple’s service answer to a worn Watch battery is a paid battery service — there is no owner procedure.
    Taptic EngineA linear resonant actuator — a weight on a spring, driven back and forth. It is why the Watch taps instead of buzzes.
    Magnetic charging interfaceThe inductive coil in the case back that mates with the puck. No port, no contacts, nothing to clean but the surface.

    Step 4 — Display assembly

    The display is the case’s front face and part of the seal. Force Touch gaskets (early models) and the display’s adhesive ring are the waterproofing.

    PartWhat this part does
    OLED display & cover crystalLTPO OLED under ion-strengthened glass or sapphire (steel/titanium models). Cracked crystal = whole-unit service territory.
    Display gasket & adhesiveThe seal. Any opening destroys it, and Apple does not publish a resealing procedure for owners.

    What is missing, in the open: everything below the module level. There is no public schematic, no parts list, no calibration procedure. The honest summary of Watch repair in 2026: screens and batteries are serviced by Apple as sealed-unit work, the SiP is a black box by design, and the FCC photos on this page are the deepest public look inside the device that exists.

    Models

    One architecture, three case families. Every model is the same idea: potted module, small cell, sealed case.

    LineYearsDefining hardwareNotes
    Series (numbered)2015–S1 → S10 SiPECG from Series 4 (FDA-cleared app); blood oxygen from Series 6 (see the record below).
    SE2020–Prior-generation SiP, no ECG/SpO2 on early modelsThe budget line — same sealed construction.
    Ultra2022–Titanium case, depth gauge, dual-frequency GPS, action buttonThe dive line; EN13319 dive-computer rating on the Oceanic+ app configuration.

    Key chips & components

    • Apple S-series SiP — no public datasheet; the module is identified only as a unit in FCC photos. The Secure Enclave public reference (site library) covers the security block inside it.
    • Ultra-wideband (U1/U2) — the precision-finding radio in the module (Series 9+/Ultra 2 use the second-generation part). Datasheet: none public — collection target logged.

    Faults — which part fails, and how you know

    A sealed device fails as a unit, so diagnosis here means identifying which sealed system died — and knowing the honest service path for each.

    • Battery life collapses — check the reported battery health in Settings; below 80% the fix is Apple’s battery service. There is no safe owner opening procedure for this glued, sealed case.
    • Heart-rate or SpO2 readings vanish — first the fit and the case-back crystal (dirt and a loose band defeat optical sensors), then software; a dead sensor array is a whole-unit fault.
    • Digital Crown sticks or scrolls by itself — debris under the crown is the classic cause; Apple’s own support guidance is a rinse under warm running water for water-resistant models. Mechanical failure beyond that is service-only.
    • Won’t charge, puck gets hot — a failing cell can swell against the display and lift it. A lifting screen on a Watch is a battery warning, not a screen fault. Stop charging it.

    Safety, before you open anything

    Do not pry a swollen Watch. The cell sits directly under the display crystal; forcing the case can puncture it. Apple’s battery service exists precisely because this device was designed with no owner-safe way in.

    Water resistance is a gasket condition, not a permanent property: it degrades with age, impacts, and any opening. A serviced-by-you Watch (if you get in at all) is no longer a swim watch, whatever the spec sheet said when it was new.

    The full record — dated, sourced, nothing smoothed over

    The Watch’s record contains the decade’s clearest case of a feature removed by a court — and then engineered back in.

    DateEventSource

    2019-08-30Apple announces a screen-replacement program for aluminum Series 2 and 3 models whose screens could crack around the edge in specific conditions.Apple service program notice

    2025-08-14Apple announces a redesigned Blood Oxygen feature — measured on the Watch, computed on the paired iPhone — restored to US Series 9, Series 10, and Ultra 2 by software update, following a new US Customs ruling.Apple Newsroom, Aug. 14, 2025

    Did you know

    • The entire computer is one part. The S-series SiP pots the processor, memory, storage, and radios in resin — the smallest “motherboard” Apple sells is also the least repairable object it makes.
    • The speaker is also a pump: after swimming, the Watch plays a series of tones that eject water from the speaker cavity.
    • A software update moved a medical sensor’s computation from the Watch to the phone to satisfy a customs ruling — the feature you own can be re-architected by litigation.
