Open it without destroying it

Most “unrepairable” devices are just glued shut. Adhesive softens with heat — a hair dryer on medium works if you don’t own a heat gun. Warm the edges for a minute or two, then work a thin plastic pick (an old guitar pick does the job) around the seam. Never use a metal screwdriver as a pry tool: it scratches boards and can puncture batteries.

The basic kit is inexpensive:

  • Plastic spudger and picks — for prying without scratching (a few dollars, or cut up an old credit card)
  • Suction cup — lifts screens and back glass once the adhesive is warm
  • Precision screwdrivers — phones use tiny pentalobe and tri-point screws; one $8 driver set covers all of them
  • Tweezers — for the tiny ribbon-cable connectors inside

Golden rule: if something won’t budge, add more heat — not more force. Cracked screens happen when people pry cold adhesive.

A note on smart-home gear

Smart bulbs and plugs are usually sonic-welded shut — opening them destroys the shell. For IoT, the software route is the better first move: many of these devices expose a serial console you can talk to without opening the case at all. See Protocol Reverse Engineering for how to do it with under $10 in parts.
