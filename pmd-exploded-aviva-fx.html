Exploded View: Invacare AVIVA FX

The AVIVA FX is Invacare’s front-wheel-drive complex-rehab chair: the front-wheel-drive version of the TDX SP2, running Dynamic’s LiNX electronics with the Ultra Low Maxx seating system, cleared by the FDA in January 2020. It is also a chair whose parts book does not exist in public. Invacare publishes no Illustrated Parts Breakdown and no spare parts catalogue for it — not for owners, not openly at all. Their service manual says it in one line: “You can find information about ordering spare parts in the spare parts catalogue.” No exploded views, no part numbers, no way to order a caster bearing without a dealer in the middle.

Coverage: manual-only. What Invacare does publish is the labeled overview in the User Manual — Figs. 4-1 and 4-2, eleven lettered parts, showing the FX itself in its two seating configurations — and a full Service Manual written for technicians, whose drive and battery figures are lettered part by part. That is everything the maker gives the person who rides in it and the technician who services it. We reproduce those figures exactly, as callout tables, and we name the gap exactly: every assembly-level parts breakdown for this chair is a source gap until Invacare publishes one. The FX shares its LiNX electronics with the TDX SP2 — the chair it was derived from — and the TDX SP2’s full parts catalogue is on this site, but the FX base is a different base, and the SP2 catalogue does not cover it.

The maker’s own warnings

Invacare prints these in the AVIVA FX User Manual (60101916-A) and the AVIVA FX Service Manual (1675507-A). The safety wording below is paraphrased into plain language, except the short lines in quotation marks, which are the maker’s exact words.

  • Do not modify the chair. “DO NOT modify this mobility device or any of its components.” Changing the chair or any of its parts can make it fail, and a failure can kill or seriously injure you.
  • The positioning strap is not a seat belt. “The seat positioning strap is NOT a seat belt. It is NOT designed to restrain the user in the event of a collision or tip over.” It only holds you in the right seated position. Never use it if it is damaged, frayed, or broken — get a replacement from your provider.
  • Use only Invacare-approved parts. Wrong or improper replacement parts can make the chair fail. Tighten all hardware securely after any adjustment, and make sure every electrical connection is secure — a loose connection can cut power without warning.
  • Keep lit cigarettes away from the seating. A lit cigarette dropped on the upholstery can start a fire that kills. Anyone using oxygen must take a lit cigarette out of their mouth before driving the chair, and keep ashtrays at a safe distance.
  • Watch the batteries. Check the charge before you drive — a dead battery can leave you stranded, so do not drive on depleted batteries. “The POSITIVE (+) RED battery cable MUST connect to the POSITIVE (+) battery terminal(s)/post(s).” “NEVER allow any of your tools and/or battery cable(s) to contact BOTH battery post(s) at the same time.” Batteries are heavy — up to 52 lb each — always use the lifting strap, and keep battery acid off skin, eyes, and clothing.
  • Do not use the Sip-n-Puff while the chair is moving. It can make the chair move unexpectedly.
  • Keep it dry. Do not expose the chair to liquids or rain, and do not drive it outside the operating conditions in the manual. The USB charger is for mobile phones only — nothing else, never in wet conditions, never if it is damaged.
  • Mind the pinch points when adjusting the chair, and keep your feet on the footplate at all times, never hanging over the edge.
  • Do not store the chair near heat, open flames, or combustible products.
  • Do not exceed the weight capacity. Going over the limit can make the chair unstable — 300 lb (136 kg) with the Captain’s seat or the non-elevating Ultra Low Maxx, 250 lb (113 kg) with the elevating seat.

Paraphrased from the User Manual, sections 2, 6, 7.4, and 13.1, and the Service Manual, sections 6.7 and 6.10; the quoted lines are verbatim.

Build order

The assemblies are laid out in the order you would build the chair: seating and frame first, drive second, batteries last. Every step is one labeled figure — or a set of figures — from the maker’s guides, followed by its parts table. The Callout column is the letter printed on the diagram. There is no part-number column on this page — Invacare publishes no part numbers for this chair, and we do not invent them.

  • Frame and seating: Step 1: The AVIVA FX, labeled by Invacare
  • Drive: Step 2: The motor/gearbox unit
  • Batteries: Step 3: The batteries and the battery door

Stage 1 — Frame and seating

Start with the chair itself. The maker’s overview shows the FX twice — once with the Ultra Low Maxx seating system (Fig. 4-1) and once with the Captain’s seat (Fig. 4-2) — and labels both with the same eleven letters. The front-wheel-drive layout is the thing to notice: the big powered wheels sit at the front, under the front of the seat, with small castors at the back and anti-tippers at the front.

Step 1 — The AVIVA FX, labeled by Invacare

The two overview diagrams (User Manual, §4.1, p. 22) are reproduced here as their callout table. No downloadable scan of the page could be hosted, so the diagrams themselves are linked: see Figs. 4-1 and 4-2 on page 22 of the public ManualsLib copy.

Stage 2 — Drive

One motor per side, each bolted to its own gearbox. The service manual treats the motor and gearbox as one unit that can be replaced together or split apart.

Step 2 — The motor/gearbox unit

Fig. 6-13 in the Service Manual (§6.7.1) labels the unit in two parts. The motors are DuraWatt 4-pole units, maintenance-free with lifetime brushes — there is no brush service in the manual. When a motor is fitted to a gearbox, the scale on the motor is lined up with the mark on the gearbox: 200° for the left motor, 350° for the right. The nut that holds the sealing ring is tightened to 8.5 Nm.

CalloutPart (maker’s label)What this part does
AGearboxThe gear set between the motor and the drive wheel. It slows the motor down and multiplies its turning force.
BMotorThe DuraWatt electric motor that turns the drive wheel — one per side, 24 volts, 324 watts.

The engaging lever — the freewheel lever that disengages the drive so the chair can be pushed by hand — has its own figures in the same section: its set screw is tightened to 6.2 Nm, and the cap screw to 1.7 Nm with thread-locking adhesive. The rubber bumper and the shock damper are separate service items in the same section (§6.7.6, §6.7.7).

Stage 3 — Batteries

The two batteries live in a box under the seat, behind a rear door that carries the electronics with it. That is the design detail that matters for service: the LiNX power module and the G-Trac sensor stay attached to the battery door assembly while you change the batteries — you lift the whole door, electronics and all, over the rear swing arm and set it on the ground.

Step 3 — The batteries and the battery door

The lettered parts below are the ones the Service Manual’s battery figures (§6.10.3, Figs. 6-41 to 6-48) mark on the battery box and its door. Two GP24 sealed gel batteries, wired in series for 24 volts; the front battery’s connector faces forward, the rear battery’s faces back, and each has a red-handled harness. The bolts that hold the battery door are tightened to 10 Nm, and the battery cable screws to 6 Nm. If new batteries go in, the battery synchronization procedure must be run within 24 hours of powering on, or the battery gauge will not read the charge level correctly — the manual sends you to the LiNX service manual for that procedure, and that manual is in this site’s library.

The chair in numbers, from the manual’s own tables

All of these come from the User Manual’s Technical Specifications (§13.1, pp. 92–96) and its ISO 7176-15 Annex A tables (§13.2, pp. 97–102):

  • Maximum user weight: 300 lb (136 kg) with the Captain’s seat or the non-elevating Ultra Low Maxx; 250 lb (113 kg) with the elevating seat.
  • Maximum speed: 5.8 mph (9.3 km/h) or 7.5 mph (12 km/h), by configuration.
  • Range (ISO 7176-4): 20.4 mi (32.8 km) at 5.8 mph; 12.7 mi (20.4 km) at 7.5 mph. The manual’s own note: range depends on terrain, temperature, driving habits, and battery condition.
  • Rated slope: 9° (15.8%), with a 300 lb (136 kg) payload.
  • Climbable obstacle: 3.25 in (83 mm) forward; 2.5 in (64 mm) in reverse.
  • Turning diameter: 53.5 in (1360 mm). Pivot width: 53.5 in (1360 mm).
  • Size: total length 51.4 in (1305 mm); maximum base width 24.25 in (616 mm); overall height 43 in (1100 mm).
  • Ground clearance: greater than 3.15 in (80 mm).
  • Weight: total mass 379.2–434.3 lb (172–197 kg).
  • Batteries: two GP24 sealed VRLA gel batteries, 73.6 Ah (C20), about 51.5 lb (23.4 kg) each; 63-amp circuit breaker. The battery lesson is on this site.
  • Motors: DuraWatt 4-pole, 24 V DC, 324 W at 13.5 amps; gearbox sound level 64 dBa.
  • Charger: 8 amps nominal, 24 volts nominal.
  • Protection: base IPX4 (water sprayed from any direction); charger IPX1 (vertically falling drops).
  • Operating conditions: 41–104 °F (5–40 °C), relative humidity 20–75%.
  • Service life: five years, used daily and in line with the manual.
  • Standards: the Annex A disclosure lists conformance to ISO 7176-1, -2, -3, -4, -5, -6, -7, -8, -10, -11, -13, -14, -15, -16, -21, -22, and -19.

One caution: the manual’s two tables do not always agree. The specification table (§13.1) gives a turning diameter of 53.5 in (1360 mm) and a forward climbable obstacle of 3.25 in (83 mm); the Annex A tables give minimum turning radii of 26.0 in (660 mm, Captain’s seat) and 26.8 in (680 mm, Ultra Low Maxx) and different obstacle figures for each seating configuration. Both are reported here as printed; the manual does not reconcile them.

If the chair will not move, the manual’s troubleshooting chapter (§12) covers driving performance, the control system, and battery and charger tips — and the LiNX fault codes are translated on this site: Fault codes, translated.

What the service documents add, and where they stop

The Service Manual (1675507-A) is written for technicians, and it covers real work: a yearly service plan (§6.5); troubleshooting for operational faults, drive faults, and charger faults (§6.4); every shroud (§6.6); the motor/gearbox unit, clutch, engaging lever, rubber bumper, shock damper, and suspension spring (§6.7); wheels, tyres, and the anti-tipper unit (§6.8); the power module, G-Trac sensor, cable harness, and software updates (§6.9); batteries, harnesses, circuit breaker, and battery doors (§6.10); lighting (§6.11); and seating (§6.12), plus the Dahl docking adaptation kit (§7). It names the tools the work needs — 4 mm and 6 mm Allen keys, a 10 mm socket and wrench, a torque wrench, and wooden blocks at least 14 × 14 × 30 cm to prop the chair.

What it does not contain is the parts list. Its own introduction (§1.2) sends the reader to the spare parts catalogue for ordering — and no spare parts catalogue for the AVIVA FX is published openly. The battery synchronization procedure it requires after a battery change lives in the LiNX service manual, not in this one — that manual is held in this site’s library.

The record on this chair

The AVIVA FX is, in the FDA’s own words, a front-wheel-drive version of the previously cleared Invacare TDX SP2 with LiNX electronics and the Ultra Low Maxx seating system — cleared 2 January 2020 as a Class II powered wheelchair (21 CFR 890.3860, product code ITI). It is a 24-volt chair with two 12-volt batteries and two 4-pole single-stage drive motors, a rigid non-folding base, two casters at the rear, and two anti-tippers at the front.

One recall touches this chair. On 12 November 2021 the FDA classified recall Z-0446-2022: AVIVA FX chairs fitted with a LiNX Gyro module (DLX-GRY100-A or DLX-GRY101-A) running firmware version 6.1.2 could decelerate harder than the programmed rate, because the system followed the wrong deceleration profile — a software-design cause, with potential injury. Invacare issued an urgent medical device letter the same day and a field correction through its recall portal: stop using firmware 6.1.2 on programming tools and stored media, and update the LiNX Access tools to the latest version. If your FX has a gyro module, check which firmware version it is running.

What is missing, in the open

Frame, drive, seating, and electronics breakdowns for this chair: not published by the maker. That is not a gap in our research — it is the gate, documented. No Illustrated Parts Breakdown or spare parts catalogue for the AVIVA FX is published openly by Invacare; the service manual that does exist points to a catalogue it does not contain; and the standing-system variant, the AVIVA FX MPS Maxx, has its own manual and is not covered here. The serial number label — Fig. 4-5 in the user manual — is the one key you hold: every part on this chair is orderable by a dealer using it. Until Invacare publishes the breakdown, a dealer’s parts screen is the only copy — and anything a dealer can see for your chair, you are entitled to ask them to show you.
