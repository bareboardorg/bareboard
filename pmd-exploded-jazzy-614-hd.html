Exploded View: Pride Jazzy 614 & 614 HD

This page is different, and the difference is the story. Pride Mobility does not publish an Illustrated Parts Breakdown for the Jazzy 614 or the Jazzy 614 HD — not for owners, not openly at all. What Pride does publish is the labeled overview in the owner’s manual — the whole chair, the power base, the electrical parts, the seat adjustments, the drive wheel taken apart, and the batteries, each with its own diagram — plus a sell sheet that names the heavy-duty model’s controller. This page puts both together, and names the gap exactly: everything below the manual — motors, gearboxes, casters, shrouds, harnesses — has no public part number, because Pride sells those parts through its dealers.

Coverage: manual-only. The Jazzy 614 is a mid-wheel-drive power chair: two in-line motors, Active-Trac suspension, and two 12-volt NF-22 batteries. Mid-wheel drive means the powered wheels sit in the middle of the chair, with a small swivelling caster wheel at each corner — that is why it turns in a tight circle. The Jazzy 614 HD is the same chair built heavier: 450 lb weight capacity instead of 300 lb, solid 14-inch drive wheels instead of pneumatic or solid, and a 50-amp Dynamic Shark controller. The controller is the electronics that drive the chair: the joystick you hold and the power module inside the base that connects it to the motors and batteries. NF-22 is a battery size standard — the size, not a brand. Weight capacity is 300 lb for the 614 and 450 lb for the 614 HD. The chair is 25.125 inches wide and, by the manual’s own drawing, turns in a 20-inch radius.

Not the Jazzy EVO 614. Pride also sells a newer, different chair called the Jazzy EVO 614 (and EVO 614HD), with 13.75-inch perforated drive wheels, a 60-amp LiNX controller, and its own manual (INFMANU5238, 2022). That chair is not covered here.

The maker’s own warnings, word for word

Pride prints these in the 614 owner’s manual. Word for word, Australian spelling and all:

An authorised Pride Provider or a qualified technician must perform the initial setup of this power chair and must perform all of the procedures in this manual.

Do not use the power chair while the drive motors are disengaged! Do not disengage the drive motors when the power chair is on an incline or decline, as the unit could roll on its own! Only engage the freewheel mode when on a level surface.

It is important to remember that when your power chair is in freewheel mode, the braking system is disengaged.

Do not use the freewheel lever handles as tie-down points to secure this product.

Explosive gases may be generated while charging the batteries. Keep the power chair and battery charger away from sources of ignition such as flames or sparks and provide adequate ventilation when charging the batteries.

The batteries in your power chair should only be serviced or replaced by an authorised Pride Provider or a qualified technician.

Do not mix old and new batteries. Always replace both batteries at the same time.

Failure to ensure that the axle key is properly installed into the axle slot when mounting the wheel can result in electronic brake failure, personal injury, and/or product damage.

Make sure pneumatic tyres are inflated to the psi/bar/kPa air pressure rating indicated on each tyre. Do not underinflate or overinflate your tyres. Low pressure may result in loss of control, and overinflated tyres may burst. Overinflating tyres can cause them to explode.

Battery posts, terminals, and related accessories contain lead and lead compounds. Wear goggles and gloves when handling batteries and wash hands after handling.

The assemblies are laid out in the order you would build the chair: the whole chair first, then the base, then the electronics, then the seat, then the wheels and the batteries. Every step is one labeled diagram from the maker’s manual, followed by its parts table. This manual labels its parts by name, not by number, so there is no callout column — the maker’s label is the first column. Stage 1 — The chair and the base

Start with the two halves. The chair has two main assemblies: the seat assembly and the power base assembly. The seat assembly is the armrests, seatback, and seat base. The power base assembly is two motor/brake assemblies, two drive wheels, four caster wheels, two batteries, and wiring harnesses. A caster wheel is a small wheel that is not powered; it swivels so the chair can turn. A shroud is a plastic cover over the base. A harness is a bundle of wires with plugs on the ends.

Step 3 — Electrical parts

The electrical parts live on or inside the power base. The main circuit breaker is on the front of the base; the power module sits inside the base. The power module is the box that connects the joystick to the motors, the batteries, and the off-board charger. The main circuit breaker is a safety switch: when the batteries and the motors are heavily strained, it trips to prevent damage. If it trips, let the chair rest for about a minute, push the breaker button back in, turn the controller on, and carry on. If it keeps tripping, call your Pride Provider.

Stage 2 — Freewheel levers

Each motor has a manual freewheel lever. The levers let you disconnect the drive motors from the gearboxes so the chair can be pushed by hand. Push both levers down to engage the motors (drive mode); pull both levers up to disengage them (freewheel mode). If a lever is hard to move, rock the chair gently back and forth and try again. Never use your foot on the levers, never stand on them, and never use the lever handles as tie-down points. And remember the warning above: in freewheel mode the brakes are off, so only ever do this on level ground with someone there to hold the chair.

Step 4 — The freewheel levers

Freewheel LeverOne on each motor. Down means the motors drive the chair; up means the motors are disconnected and the chair rolls freely — with no brakes. The diagram carries no printed label; the name is from the manual’s own text.

Stage 3 — The seat

The seat slides onto the power base on the Universal Mounting System: two aluminium rails (extrusions) under the seat that slide onto the trapeze bars, and a seat latch safety you flip down to lock it. Never pick the seat frame up by the armrests — they pivot freely and you can lose control of the seat. The controller cable is the fragile part of this job: lift the armrest straight up first, route the cable under the armrest or toward the inside of the armrest pad, never on the outside, and tie it down so it cannot catch in the wheels, get pinched in the seat frame, or snag in a doorway.

Step 5 — Assembly view

One rule from the manual: any nylon insert lock nut you remove must be replaced with a new one — never reuse it, because the nylon insert is damaged by removal and the nut will not hold as tightly.

Plug the controller connector into the power base, then secure the controller harness to the armrest receiver with one or more wire ties. Lift the armrest straight up before tying the cable down, and route it under the armrest or toward the inside of the armrest pad — never on the outside.

Step 8 — Seat height

You can change the seat height to one of four positions by raising the front and rear trapeze bars. Raise or lower only one trapeze bar (front or rear) and you also change the seat base angle — the dump, the tilt of the seat. The job needs a metric/standard socket set and ratchet, an adjustable spanner, and thread lock. Thread lock is a liquid that stops screws working loose. Turn the controller off, disconnect it, flip up the seat latch safety, squeeze the seat latch, slide the seat forward and off, loosen the setscrews on the seat towers and the screws on the trapeze bars, pull the retaining clips, move the bars, and put it all back — new thread lock on every screw you removed, retaining clips back in, setscrews tight, seat back on, controller reconnected, harness tied down. Remove the occupant from the chair before making any adjustment.

You can move the seat forward or rearward by changing the extrusion mounting position: unplug the controller, remove the seat, take both extrusions off the bottom of the seat, and refasten them on a different set of mounting holes. You must move both extrusions the same number of holes, either forward or rearwards.

Seat Mounting HolesThe row of holes under the seat; moving both extrusions the same number of holes moves the seat forward or back.

Step 10 — Seatback and armrests

If your chair has an adjustable seatback, it sets to four angles: 90°, 102°, 105°, or 107°. Remove the adjusting screws from each seat hinge, set the back, and refit the screws. If it has a reclining seat, pull up on the seatback release lever on the right side of the seat base, lean to the angle you want, and release the lever. Each armrest’s width changes independently — loosen the two knobs on each side of the armrest receiver bracket, slide the armrest in or out, and tighten. Changing the armrest width may increase the overall width of your chair.

Step 11 — Under the armrest

You can move the controller in toward or out away from the armrest, or move it to the other armrest for left-hand or right-hand use. Flip the armrest up so it is perpendicular to the floor, loosen the setscrew on the controller bracket, slide the controller to the position you want, and tighten the setscrew. To change sides: turn the controller off, unplug it, cut the wire ties, slide it out, and fit it into the other armrest. Do not place the controller harness where it can be pinched in the seat frame or the power base frame.

The foot platform adjusts three ways. Stage 4 — Charging, wheels, and batteries

The chair uses two long-lasting, 12-volt, deep-cycle batteries. Deep-cycle means the battery is built to be drained down and recharged over and over, unlike a car battery. They are sealed and maintenance free — there is no fluid level to check. AGM (absorbed glass mat) and Gel-Cell are the two sealed deep-cycle types Pride accepts; both are sealed and maintenance-free. Never use a car battery charger — use only the supplied 5-amp off-board charger.

Step 13 — Battery charging

To charge: position the front of the chair next to a standard electrical outlet, switch the controller off, plug the off-board charger into the charger/programming socket on the controller, then plug the charger into the wall. When the batteries are fully charged, unplug the charger from the wall first, then from the controller. Charge 8 to 14 hours after daily use. If you use the chair once a week or less, charge at least once a week for 12 to 14 hours. Never charge for more than 24 hours in one charging cycle. The charger takes the wall voltage of 120 VAC (alternating current) and converts it to 24 VDC (direct current) for the batteries. The charger will not work at all once the batteries have dropped to nearly zero voltage; if that happens, call your Pride Provider. New batteries need breaking in: fully recharge before first use, run the chair gently, recharge for 8 to 14 hours, and repeat — after four or five charging cycles they reach 100%.

Step 14 — The drive wheel, apart

If you have pneumatic tyres and you have a flat, you can replace the tube. If your chair has a solid tyre insert, you must replace the entire wheel assembly. The wheels should only be serviced or replaced by an authorised Pride Provider or a qualified technician. The steps, from the manual: turn the controller off, put the chair up on blocks, and if you are changing a pneumatic tyre, completely deflate it before removing the wheel. Remove the drive wheel nut and washer from the axle, pull the wheel off, remove the screws from the rim assembly, and separate the front and rear rim. Replace the tube, or the whole assembly if it is a solid tyre. Reassemble the rims, refit the screws, slide the wheel back onto the axle — making sure the axle key is in the axle slot — and refit the nut and washer and tighten them. Inflate pneumatic tyres to the pressure rating printed on each tyre, and take the chair off the blocks.

Step 15 — Battery installation

To replace the batteries: turn the controller off, make sure the chair is in drive mode, pull off the rear shroud, push down on the disconnect release button and unplug the rear battery harness, unfasten the battery strap, and slide the rear battery out. Do the same for the front battery. Fit a battery harness onto each new battery following the wiring diagram on the decal — red wire to the red positive (+) terminal, black wire to the black negative (−) terminal — and tighten the fasteners so the connections are secure. Put the batteries back in, fasten the strap around the rear battery, plug each harness into its mating plug on the power base, refit the rear shroud, and charge the batteries. The batteries sit flat in the tray with their terminals facing inward, toward each other. Each battery weighs 38 lb; get help lifting them. Never replace a battery while the seat is occupied, and never mix an old battery with a new one — replace both at the same time.

The full record

Recalls. No FDA recall names the Jazzy 614 or the Jazzy 614 HD (FDA device recalls database, searched 23 November 2026).

Clearance. No 510(k) in the FDA’s database names the Jazzy 614 or the Jazzy 614 HD (searched 23 November 2026). The 510(k) number most often tied to this family, K042612, names the device JAZZY 600, cleared 6 October 2004 — that is the Jazzy 600, not this chair — so this page does not claim it as this chair’s clearance. The 510(k) summary is in the library: K042612.pdf. The database entry is at FDA accessdata, K042612.

Adverse-event report. One report is on file with the FDA for a Jazzy 614 HD (MDR Report Key 5045360, report MW5055843, a voluntary report). The event was 5 August 2015 and the report reached the FDA 26 August 2015. The patient problem is listed as “Physical Entrapment” and the outcome as “Life Threatening”; the product problem is listed as “Device-Device Incompatibility”. The record carries no narrative text, so there is no more detail to give. An adverse-event report is an allegation, not a finding — it is included here because the full record includes it. What is missing, in the open

Motor and gearbox breakdowns, caster and wheel breakdowns, shroud and frame breakdowns, harness part numbers, and an order form with part numbers for this chair: not published by the maker. That is not a gap in our research — it is the gate, documented. Pride distributes parts through its dealers, and the owner’s manual is the only public document that names the parts at all. The serial number label on your chair is the one key you hold: every part on this chair is orderable by a dealer using it. Until Pride publishes the breakdown, a dealer’s parts screen is the only copy — and anything a dealer can see for your chair, you are entitled to ask them to show you.
