Quantum Q6 Edge 3, Opened — Every Part, Named

This page takes the Quantum Q6 Edge 3 apart on paper — every assembly, every part, named. It is built from Quantum’s own Illustrated Parts Breakdown, callout by callout, with Quantum’s part numbers and a plain-language line for every part. If your chair is an Edge 3, this is the map of it.

How to use this page: open the Q6 Edge 3 IPB to the page number given under each table. The IPB’s diagram shows the assembly pulled apart, each part marked with a callout letter and number. Find the callout in the table below it: that row gives you Quantum’s part number, the part’s name, how many the chair takes, and what it does, in one plain line.

One warning before you start: this IPB is the Australian edition (serial JE7, September 2020). Part numbers for a US chair can differ. Check your chair’s serial number and confirm the part number with the dealer or on Quantum’s parts lookup before you order. What never differs is the shape of the chair: the assemblies, and what each part does, are the same.

About this chair

The Q6 Edge 3 is Quantum Rehab’s complex-rehabilitation power base — the chair underneath the seating that clinics fit to the rider. Its funding codes are K0848, K0849, K0856, K0857, and K0861 (Quantum’s code reference). It was sold with three electronics families: Q-Logic 3 (built by Curtis Instruments), and the simpler NE and NE+ families (built on Dynamic Controls’ LiNX system). The motors are rated for 6 miles per hour, standard or ACCU-TRAC. It runs on two NF-22 batteries. An iLevel seat lift was available.

The source for this page is Quantum’s Illustrated Parts Breakdown for the chair, document INFMANU4994 Revision C, exported 8 September 2020. A parts breakdown — an IPB — is the manufacturer’s own list of every serviceable part, with a drawing of each assembly pulled apart and a table of callouts, part numbers, names, and quantities.

How to read this exploded view

  • Callout — the letter-and-number mark on the IPB’s drawing (A1, B1, J1). Find it on the drawing, find it in the table.
  • Parent assembly — a callout range like A1–A3 means one part number buys the whole assembly: the part plus everything listed under it. The IPB says most parts live inside assemblies and may not be sold separately.
  • No callout (—) — the IPB lists the part as “N/A”: paperwork and labels that have no place on a drawing.
  • Qty — how many of that part one chair takes.
  • What it is — Quantum’s own name for the part, word for word from the IPB.
  • What it does — our plain-language line, written for this page. It says what the part type does, not which exact parts it joins to — the IPB does not say that, so neither do we.
  • Left and right are from the rider’s point of view, sitting in the chair.
  • Colors are Quantum’s names (Back in Black, Red Corvette, Cherry Bomb). The IPB’s color chart is not reproduced here; every color appears in the shroud and wheel-insert tables with its own part number.

The drive motors are the two motors that turn the big wheels. Each side has its own motor, its own mount, and its own suspension. What to do with a part number

  1. Write down the callout, the part number, and your chair’s serial number. The serial number is on the chair’s label, usually on the base.
  2. Look the number up on Quantum’s parts lookup, prideprovider.com — the IPB itself points there as the up-to-date lookup — or give it to the dealer and ask them to confirm it against your serial number.
  3. This IPB export lists no prices. Ask for the current price before you agree to anything, and ask whether the part is sold on its own or only as its parent assembly.
  4. Before you order a battery or a charger, check the chemistry: gel and AGM batteries take different charger settings, and the wrong setting shortens the batteries’ life. The charger table says which setting each charger takes.

Safety

Switch the chair OFF and unplug the charger before you touch any part. The batteries stay live even with the chair switched off: never let a metal tool touch two battery terminals at once, and never let a wrench touch a terminal and the frame at the same time.

The IPB itself prints no prose safety warnings. On this chair the warnings ride as decals — and the IPB lists them as parts in the decal table, including the battery-warnings decal. Keep those decals readable: they are the warning system.

The chair’s own warning labels are listed in the decal table: battery warnings, controller cord placement, deflate the tire completely, EMI-RFI (radio interference), and the extension-cord and grounding-prong caution. The full safety text lives in the Consumer Safety Guide that comes with the chair (document INFMANU3992, listed in the owner’s package). This site does not hold a copy of that guide yet, so its warnings are not quoted here — read the guide that came with your chair, and read the labels on the chair itself.

One instruction from the IPB is quoted verbatim above, on the front suspension: the dampers must be replaced in pairs. Do not replace one side only.

Words we use, defined

  • IPB — Illustrated Parts Breakdown: the manufacturer’s own list of every serviceable part, with a drawing of each assembly pulled apart.
  • Callout — the letter-and-number mark on the drawing that points at one part.
  • Parent assembly — one part number that buys a whole group of parts together (a callout range, like A1–A3).
  • NF-22 — a battery size. Your chair takes two of them.
  • Gel / AGM — the two kinds of sealed lead-acid battery. The charger must be set to match.
  • Q-Logic 3 — the chair’s electronics family, built by Curtis Instruments. Q-Logic 3e is its joystick variant.
  • NE / NE+ — Quantum’s simpler electronics families, built on Dynamic Controls’ LiNX system. The IPB spells it “LINIX”.
  • ACCU-TRAC — Quantum’s motor option with an encoder, a sensor that reports how far the wheel has turned, so the chair drives straighter.
  • iLevel — Quantum’s seat lift: it raises the seat so you can reach things and talk eye to eye.
  • PTO — power take-off: an output of the electronics that drives something other than the wheels.
  • Inhibit — the chair’s “do not drive” signal, sent while the charger is plugged in.
  • Freewheel — the release that lets the chair be pushed by hand when the motors are off.
