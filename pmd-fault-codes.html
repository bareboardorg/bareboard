Fault Codes, Translated

Your chair already tells you what is wrong. It shows a flashing light or a code on the screen — and nobody ever gave you the list that explains it. This is the list.

Three control systems cover most power chairs. Find yours, read the code the way the chair shows it, and start with the owner checks. Write the code down before you call anyone: a code you can name is a repair you can argue about.

Provenance: these tables are compiled from the manufacturers’ own published manuals — the PG Drives VR2 and R-net technical manuals and the Dynamic Controls LiNX manuals — and checked line by line against them (R-net Technical Manual SK77981-14; PP1A Programmer Guide SK73747). Codes that could not be confirmed in the manuals are not listed. Documentation, not bench measurement: if your chair disagrees with this page, the chair wins — write down what yours actually does.

Curtiss-Wright VR2 & VSI — count the flashing bars

When a VR2 or VSI chair trips, the battery gauge lights flash. Count how many bars are flashing.

Flashing barsWhat it meansWhat you check first
1 barLow battery voltageCharge the batteries overnight. Check the main battery plugs and the circuit breaker/fuse. If it keeps happening, load-test each 12V battery for a dead cell (see Cheap Fixes, Procedure 3).
2 barsLeft motor disconnectedThe controller cannot see the left drive motor. Check the heavy motor plug at the power module, look for a pinched or cut cable under the seat, and check the motor brushes.
3 barsLeft motor wiring trip (short)A left motor wire is shorting to battery power. Inspect the cable where it passes frame hinges. Unplug the left motor: if the fault clears, the short is in the motor or its cable.
4 barsRight motor disconnectedSame checks as 2 bars, on the right motor: plug, cable, brushes.
5 barsRight motor wiring trip (short)Same checks as 3 bars, on the right motor cable.
6 barsInhibit active (charger connected)The chair thinks it must not drive — usually because the charger is plugged in. Unplug the charger. If it is already unplugged, check the charger socket for a bent pin or dirt (Procedure 2), and check for a stuck seating lockout switch.
7 barsJoystick fault at power-upUsually your own hand: something was resting on the joystick when you switched on. Switch off, take everything off the stick, let it center, switch on without touching it.
7 bars + S lightBus communication faultThe joystick and the power module lost contact. Unplug the round bus cable between them, check the pins for corrosion or bends, and plug it firmly back in (Procedure 4).
8 barsControl system / power module tripThe power module failed its own self-check. Check every heavy power and bus connection, switch off for 30 seconds, and try again. If it persists, the fault is inside the power module.
8 bars + A lightActuator / seating tripA fault in a power seating actuator (tilt, recline, elevate, legrest). Inspect the 2-pin actuator plugs; unplug actuators one at a time to find the bad one.
9 barsParking brake faultThe most owner-fixable code on this page. One or both freewheel levers are in the PUSH position, or a brake plug is loose. Flip both levers firmly to DRIVE and power-cycle (Procedure 1).
10 bars (all flashing)High battery voltageToo much voltage reached the controller — a faulty charger, or loose battery terminals spiking on a downhill. Tighten the terminal bolts and check battery health.

Curtiss-Wright R-net — read the screen

On R-net chairs with a color screen (CJSM, CJSM2, Omni), a trip shows three things: the module that complained (PM = power module, JSM = joystick module, ISM = seating module), a short text, and a 4-digit code. The code is the exact sub-circuit. Here are the ones an owner can act on.

CodeModuleWhat it meansWhat you check first
1505 / 1506PMParking brake error (left / right brake)Check both freewheel levers are in DRIVE; inspect the small 2-pin brake wires at the back of each motor (Procedure 1).
1D05JSMJoystick stationary time exceededThe joystick was held in one position too long. Power-cycle the chair — this code clears on restart. If it returns at once, check that the stick springs back freely to center; a binding boot or gaiter can hold it off-center.
2C00PMLow battery voltageCharge; check the main disconnect plug and terminal bolts for looseness or corrosion.
2F00JSMJoystick deflected at power-upThe stick was pushed during boot. Center it; if it will not clear, power off and on, hands free.
3B00PMLeft motor disconnectedCheck the left motor plug and brushes.
3C00PMRight motor disconnectedCheck the right motor plug and brushes.
3D00 / 3D01PMLeft motor short circuitShort in the left motor cable or winding. Inspect the cable run.
3E00 / 3E01PMRight motor short circuitShort in the right motor cable or winding. Inspect the cable run.
4401PMInternal controller tripPower-cycle after 30 seconds off. If it persists, the power module needs replacement.
Trip text: Over-currentPM / ISMActuator channel over-currentUnplug seating actuators one by one to isolate the shorted cable.

Dynamic Controls LiNX — count the red flashes

On LiNX chairs, the light inside the round on/off button flashes red: a number of flashes, a pause, then it repeats. Count the flashes.

Red flashesWhat it meansWhat you check first
All speed LEDs rippleOut of neutral at power-upThe joystick was held off-center at switch-on. Let it center — the fault clears by itself.
1 flashRemote / joystick errorCheck the bus cable into the joystick: unplug, inspect the pins, reconnect.
2 flashesNetwork or configuration errorCheck every bus plug; charge the batteries (low voltage below cut-off triggers this too). A replacement module fitted without configuration also causes it.
3 flashesLeft motor faultOpen or short circuit on the left motor, its cable, or its connector.
4 flashesRight motor faultOpen or short circuit on the right motor, its cable, or its connector.
5 flashesLeft park brake faultLeft brake open/short — or the left freewheel lever is in push mode. Flip it to DRIVE; check the 2-pin brake plug.
6 flashesRight park brake faultRight brake open/short — or the right freewheel lever is in push mode. Flip it to DRIVE; check the 2-pin brake plug.
7 flashesModule errorIncludes: chair stalled against an obstacle (reverse away, cycle power), or the chair was pushed while switched off (cycle power to clear), or a power module fault.

Before you call anyone

Read the code. Write it down with the date. Try the owner check in the table — the most common codes on this page (brake levers, charger inhibit, a loose bus plug, a dying battery) are fixed in minutes with no tools or a $10 meter. If the fault is inside a module, you now know which module, and you can say so.
