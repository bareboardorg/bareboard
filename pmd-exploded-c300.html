    Permobil C300, Opened — Every Part, Named

    This page takes the Permobil C300 apart on paper — every assembly, every part, named. It is built from Permobil’s own parts catalog, callout by callout, with Permobil’s part numbers and a plain-language line for every part. The C300 is a front-wheel-drive complex-rehabilitation power base; this page covers the base and its seating — PS, Corpus II, and Corpus 3G — across all four electronics generations the chair was sold with: VSI, Pilot+, VR2, and R-net.

    How to use this page: open the catalog page linked under each table. The catalog’s diagram shows the assembly pulled apart, each part marked with a numbered callout. Find the callout in the table below it: that row gives you Permobil’s part number, the part’s number in Permobil’s current ordering system, the part’s name, and what it does, in one plain line.

    One warning before you start: four electronics generations fit this chair — VSI, Pilot+, VR2, and R-net — so find which one your chair has before ordering electronics. And the chassis covers changed in January 2012: the covers are split into two assemblies, before and after that date. The headrest assembly is the one exception on this page: its catalog table is a list of 6,615 image-only entries with no descriptions, and it is not reproduced as a table — see that assembly for why.

    About this chair

    The C300 is Permobil’s front-wheel-drive complex-rehabilitation power base — the chair underneath the seating that clinics fit to the rider. Permobil’s own words on the Corpus 3G sheet: the company calls itself the inventor of front-wheel-drive powerchair technology. The drive wheels sit at the front, with the casters behind — the layout that lets the chair turn in tight indoor spaces and climb obstacles with the big wheels first.

    Seating came three ways: the PS seat (adjustable, VR2 as standard), the Corpus II seat, and the Corpus 3G seat with its ergonomic backrest. Power functions run to 50° of tilt and 175° of recline on the Corpus 3G sheet; the PS seat’s elevator lifts 8 inches.

    The electronics changed across the chair’s production: VSI, Pilot+, VR2, and R-net with the ICS seating bus. The R-net configuration sheets in this catalog are dated by the chairs they shipped on: the PS sheet covers chairs shipped on or after 14 April 2008, the Corpus sheet chairs shipped on or after 19 August 2008. Both use an 80-amp PG R-net power module.

    It runs on two Group 34 batteries and carries up to 300 lb. Its funding codes are K0856 and K0861.

    The source for this page is Permobil’s own parts catalog at catpages.permobil.com, fetched 22 November 2026: 36 category pages for the C300, each with a diagram and a parts table. A parts catalog is the manufacturer’s own list of every serviceable part, with a drawing of each assembly pulled apart and a table of callouts, part numbers, and names.

    How to read this exploded view

    • Pos — the catalog’s position code. The number before the dot is the callout on the diagram; the number after the dot separates rows at the same callout. The same callout can carry two rows when two versions fit there — callout 9 on the old chassis lists the brake-release arm for pre-2012 chairs and for chairs after 2013 — and the same part can appear at two callouts when the chair takes two of them.
    • A callout with a list under it on the diagram (like “1: 2, 17–27”) is a parent assembly — the parts listed under it belong to it.
    • Permobil part no. — the catalog number.
    • D365 part no. — the part’s number in Permobil’s Dynamics 365 ordering system, as the catalog lists it. Where the catalog shows one, that is the current ordering number. Where it is blank, the catalog lists only the catalog number.
    • What it is — the catalog’s own description, word for word.
    • What it does — our plain-language line, written for this page. It says what the part type does, not which exact parts it joins to — the catalog does not say that, so neither do we.
    • No quantities. The catalog lists parts per assembly without counts, so this page lists none.
    • OBSOLETE — the maker no longer sells the part. Order the replacement the catalog names, if any.
    • Left and right are from the rider’s point of view, sitting in the chair.
    • The headrest assembly is the exception. Its catalog table lists 6,615 entries, every one marked “Image Only” with no position and no description — a list of headrest pad variants by part code. They are not reproduced as a table here; the assembly section below says so and links the catalog page.

    What the service manual adds

    The parts catalog names the parts. The service manual — Permobil’s own, Edition no. 3, UK — says how they come apart and what to torque them to. This section is from that manual, held in the library. Page numbers are the manual’s own.

    Torques

    • Front wheel bolt, 33 Nm (manual p. 12). The bolt must be used once only; a removed bolt is not allowed to be refitted. No other type of bolt or washer is to be used, and no lubrication in the threaded hole in the axle or on the bolt. If the wheel is tight, use a puller; a rubber mallet is allowed, but hitting too hard could damage the gear.
    • Rear wheel nut, 24 Nm (manual p. 13). Screws and nuts must only be used once; once removed, they must never be refitted. No other type of screw, nut, or washer than those stated may be used.
    • Wheel fork nut, 33 Nm; friction plate screw, 15 Nm (manual p. 15). Lubricate the friction-plate O-ring with the lubricant the manual names, and no other type.
    • Link arm (slewing bracket) screw, 15 Nm (manual p. 17). Do not use a pneumatic impact wrench.
    • Shock absorber spring: never adjust the spring to a setting less than 46 mm. The standard setting, for users above 100 kg, is 48 mm (manual p. 16). Adjust with no load in the wheelchair.

    Fuses

    • Main fuse, in its holder inside the front battery cover (manual p. 34). If the main fuse blows, there is often a major electrical fault; check the cause carefully before replacing it.
    • SLS fuse (Pilot+ only), in its holder inside the rear battery cover (manual p. 35).
    • Seat and lighting fuses (Pilot+ only): two fuses on the SLS drive stage, F1 (24V unswitched) and F2 (24V switched), both 15A. The seat and lighting run off the switched outlet (manual p. 35).

    Batteries

    Two batteries: the front battery lifts out with the battery belt, terminals at the front; the rear battery the same way, terminals at the rear. Leave the battery belt on the battery. The pair is covered in Batteries & Chargers — two 12-volt batteries wired in series to make 24 volts, always replaced as a matched pair.

    Faults: the blinking lights

    Every time the chair starts up, it checks parts of its electronics. If a fault is found, the battery-voltage indicator on the control panel blinks rapidly, 1 to 10 lights. The number of blinking lights says what the problem could be (manual pp. 37–39):

    Blinking lightsCause

    9Failure in the wheel lock circuit

    The manual’s own example: lights 1–7 — 3 red and 4 orange — blinking rapidly on start-up means a control-panel fault. Its solution: make sure the joystick isn’t actuated at switch-on; otherwise change the control panel. Fault signals are not displayed while the chair is being driven; they appear the next time the chair is restarted. The R-net, VSI, and Pilot+ fault codes are translated on Fault Codes, Translated.

    Manual operation

    If the seat lift does not work because the batteries are discharged or the actuator is defective, the seat can be raised or lowered manually with the seat lift crank supplied (manual p. 22). The manual’s warning is quoted in the Safety section below.

    The full record

    No 510(k) clearance was found for the Permobil C300 in the FDA’s database (checked through OpenFDA, 22 November 2026). The nearest clearance, K071650, names the C350 powered wheelchair, cleared 12 September 2007 — it is not this chair’s clearance, and this page does not claim it as one. The K-numbers printed on the recall records below (K041219, K071650, K991658, K123290) name other Permobil chairs; they are the recalls’ listed numbers, not the C300’s.

    Three FDA recalls name the C300. All three are terminated:

    • Z-0005-2018 and Z-0006-2018, initiated 1 September 2017: potential failure of the top-plate assembly, which connects the seating system to the base. Z-0005-2018 covers the C300 Corpus II (with the C350, C400, and C500 Corpus II chairs); Z-0006-2018 covers the C300 base itself (with the C350, C400, and C500 bases). Both terminated 4 May 2021.
    • Z-3178-2018, initiated 15 August 2018: potential failure of one or both backrest telescoping bars; if they fail the user could fall back. Covers the C300 PS (with the M300 PS, C350 PS, C500 PS, and C400 PS). Terminated 3 May 2021.

    Adverse-event reports. 87 reports in the FDA’s MAUDE database name the Permobil C300 (checked through OpenFDA, 22 November 2026), from 2007 to 2025: 56 malfunctions, 26 injuries, 1 death, and 4 with no event type recorded. The peak year was 2017, with 19 reports. These are reports as filed — allegations, not findings; the database does not say the chair caused what was reported:

    • 24 October 2017, death (report 1221084-2017-00091, event 26 July 2017): the end-user was found deceased seated in the device. The circumstances are unclear in the manufacturer’s narrative, the device was not available for evaluation, and the report was filed by an attorney.
    • 9 June 2009, injury (report 1221084-2009-00001): the chair stopped suddenly going down a 12% slope and the rider, not wearing a positioning belt, fell out — a bump on the head and a broken leg. The manufacturer noted the belt would have held him in.
    • 1 August 2024, injury (report 1221084-2025-00004): the chair tipped over forward completely, trapping the rider underneath; four responders lifted it off. Bilateral tibia fractures.
    • 17 March 2023, injury (report 1221084-2023-00008): a caregiver bumped the joystick during a transfer to bed; the chair moved and collided with the side of the bed, an injury requiring medical intervention.

    Safety

    Switch the chair OFF and unplug the charger before you touch any part. The batteries stay live even with the chair switched off: never let a metal tool touch two battery terminals at once, and never let a wrench touch a terminal and the frame at the same time.

    The service manual’s own warnings, quoted word for word:

    • “Be careful when using metal objects when working with batteries. A short-circuit can easily cause an explosion. Always use safety gloves and safety goggles.”
    • “Removing the support wheels entails an increased risk of the wheelchair tipping over. The wheelchair must not be driven when the support wheels are not fitted.”
    • “The seat must not be subjected to load and the wheelchair must not be driven with the fixed seat tube in the service position.”
    • “Before pressing the service position lock in, lift up and hold the seat in a steady grip, if not, the seat will fall down which can cause personal injury”
    • “Drills must not be used in connection with manual operation of the seat lift. There is a risk of damage to materials.”
    • “Do not use a Pneumatic impact wrench.” (slewing brackets)
    • “No other type of screws, nuts and washers than those stated here may be used.” (rear wheels)
    • “Other types of bolts or washers are not to be used.” (front wheels)
    • “Do not use any type of lubrication in the threaded hole in the axle or on the bolt.” (front wheels)
    • “If the main fuse blows, there is often a major electrical fault. The cause of the fault should be checked carefully before the fuse is replaced.”
    • “The remedying of errors that are indicated via the battery voltage indicator must be performed by a person with sufficient expertise to be able to perform such in a professional manner. Always contact an authorized serviceman when in doubt.”

    The manual also says, twice: the seat is heavy and should be lifted by two persons, with care for the cabling.

    Words we use, defined

    • Parts catalog — the manufacturer’s own list of every serviceable part, with a drawing of each assembly pulled apart.
    • Pos / callout — the number on the diagram that points at one part; the catalog’s Pos code adds a dot and a second number to separate rows at the same callout.
    • D365 — Permobil’s Dynamics 365 ordering system. The D365 part number is the current ordering number.
    • Parent assembly — one callout that buys a whole group of parts together (a callout with a list under it on the diagram).
    • OBSOLETE — the maker no longer sells the part.
    • Front-wheel drive — the powered wheels sit at the front of the chair, with the casters behind.
    • VSI / Pilot+ / VR2 / R-net — the four control-system generations this chair was sold with. VSI packs the controller into the joystick panel; Pilot+ uses a separate controller and SLS drive stage; VR2 uses a power module; R-net uses a power module plus a bus that links the joystick, seating, and other modules.
    • ICS — Permobil’s seating bus: the wiring and modules that drive the power seat functions (tilt, recline, lift, legrest) on R-net chairs.
    • Corpus II / Corpus 3G — Permobil’s seating systems. Corpus 3G is the later one, with the ergonomic backrest.
    • PS seating — Permobil’s adjustable seating, built on one-inch tubing for aftermarket options.
    • Group 34 — a battery size. Your chair takes two of them.
    • HCPCS — the funding codes Medicare and insurers use for the chair: K0856 and K0861 for this base.
    • SLS drive stage — the Pilot+ drive stage: the box that takes the controller’s commands and drives the motors and seat functions.
    • SoftPot — a position sensor: a strip that reports how far the seat has moved.
    • MAUDE — the FDA’s database of adverse-event reports for medical devices. Reports are filed by makers, users, and others; they are allegations, not findings.
