    AirPods

    A battery with a radio attached, twice, in a box with a third battery. The AirPods are the end-state of anti-repair design: ultrasonically welded, glued, zero screws — and, in the Pro 2, an FDA-authorized hearing aid hiding inside the same sealed shell.

    FCC ID

    FCC IDs vary by model; the buds and the case file separately. Apple’s grantee code is BCG. Each earbud is its own radio device (Bluetooth, and ultra-wideband in the case on Pro 2), so each files its own internal photos — the only sanctioned look inside a product Apple never intends to be opened.

    Every radio a device carries — Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular, ultra-wideband, NFC — needs an FCC equipment authorization before the device can be sold in the United States. To get one, the manufacturer files photographs and test reports with the FCC, and those filings are public record. Check the label on your specific unit and look it up at fccid.io.

    What the FCC filing revealed

    The FCC filing for this device includes the documents manufacturers are required to submit: external photos, internal photos, test setup photos, RF test reports, and the user manual. These are public record at fccid.io.

    • Internal photos — the folded flex board in each bud, the button cell in the stem, the driver, and the case’s own board and coil
    • Test reports — SAR and RF exposure for a transmitter worn in the ear canal’s entrance
    • Label photos — which show the model splits (left and right buds are different models with different FCC IDs)

    What the FCC filing does not include: schematics (withheld under confidentiality requests where the maker files them), firmware, and the full bill of materials.

    Exploded view — the assemblies in build order

    This is the centerpiece of the page: the device taken apart into its assemblies, in the order they go together, every part named in plain language with what it does. An exploded view is the drawing a manufacturer makes showing every part separated along the path it assembles — the single most useful document in repair, and the one most often kept behind a dealer login.

    The source

    Parts book: none, and none is possible — there is no published procedure because there is no non-destructive way in. FCC internal photos are the source: they show the stack below. The shell is ultrasonically welded (the plastic is melted together at a seam), the battery is glued, and every teardown on record destroys the unit it opens. This exploded view is therefore a map of a device you can understand completely and service almost not at all — which is the lesson.

    Coverage: PARTIAL — no public parts book; FCC internal-photo basis. Exploded coverage of a sealed unit: complete as documentation, near-zero as a repair path, and the page says so rather than pretending otherwise.

    Build order

    Step 1 — The earbud shell & acoustics

    One welded plastic shell per ear. Inside it, everything is folded into the space behind the speaker.

    PartWhat this part does
    Shell (ultrasonically welded)Two plastic halves melted into one. There is no seam to split that closes again.
    Dynamic driverThe speaker — an 11 mm custom driver on Pro models. It is the largest single part in the bud.
    Microphones (beamforming + inward-facing)Pro models add an inward-facing mic that listens to what you hear, feeding the adaptive audio and the hearing-test feature.
    Optical / skin-detect sensorsDetect that the bud is in an ear, to play and pause.

    Step 2 — The stem — battery and brains

    The stem is not a handle. It is the battery tube and the antenna.

    PartWhat this part does
    Button cell (in the stem)A small cylindrical lithium cell, glued in place. It is the part that wears out in 2–4 years and cannot be replaced without destroying the bud.
    H1 / H2 chipApple’s headphone SoC — Bluetooth radio, audio DSP (digital signal processing), and the neural engine cores that run computational audio. No public datasheet.
    Bluetooth antennaRuns the length of the stem — which is why the stem exists.
    Force sensor / touch surfaceThe squeeze and swipe input on the stem’s flat face.

    Step 3 — The case — a third device

    The case is not packaging. It is a battery pack, a charger, and on Pro 2 a radio beacon of its own.

    PartWhat this part does
    Case batteryA larger lithium cell that recharges the buds ~4 times. Also glued; also not sold as a part.
    Charging coil & portInductive charging (Qi/MagSafe/Watch-puck on Pro 2) plus USB-C or Lightning on the port models.
    U1 ultra-wideband chip (Pro 2 case)Lets Find My point at the case with an arrow; the case also gains a speaker for Find My sounds and a lanyard loop.
    Pairing button & status LEDThe case’s only control and display.

    What is missing, in the open: any legitimate repair path for the buds themselves. Apple’s service answer for a dead bud is a replacement bud at a set fee. The honest repair content for AirPods is preventive: keep the cells cycling, store them in the case, and know that the product’s lifespan is the life of its smallest glued battery.

    Models

    Four generations of the standard bud and two of the Pro; one architecture throughout.

    LineYearsChipNotes
    AirPods (1st–4th gen)2016–W1 → H1 → H24th gen (2024) adds an ANC (active noise cancelling) variant without silicone tips.
    AirPods Pro (1st–2nd gen)2019–H1 → H2Pro 2 case adds UWB + speaker; Pro 2 gains the FDA-authorized hearing features (2024).
    AirPods Max2020–H1 (each cup)The exception: replaceable ear cushions and a headband — still no battery service path.

    Key chips & components

    • Apple H1 / H2 — headphone SoC; no public datasheet (collection target logged). The Secure Enclave reference (site library) covers the pairing security model the buds use with iCloud.
    • Apple U1 (Pro 2 case) — ultra-wideband ranging radio; no public datasheet. Collection target logged.

    Faults — which part fails, and how you know

    Diagnosis on a sealed device is triage: which of the three devices (left bud, right bud, case) is lying to you?

    • One bud drains far faster than the other — that bud’s stem cell is worn. There is no replacement; the service path is a replacement bud. A bud that dies in minutes has a failed cell, not a software bug.
    • Crackling / static / weak bass (Pro, 1st gen) — the documented manufacturing defect Apple ran a service program for (see the record). Check program eligibility before paying for a replacement.
    • Case won’t charge the buds — dirty contacts in the case wells are the first cause (the buds’ stem contacts oxidize); a case that itself won’t charge points at its port or coil.
    • Find My can’t locate a bud — buds only report when awake and near a Bluetooth listener; a fully dead bud is invisible until charged. The Pro 2 case (with its own U1 radio and speaker) is findable on its own.

    Safety, before you open anything

    Do not attempt to open a bud with a swollen or hot cell. The cell is the stem; cutting the stem is cutting the battery. Swollen AirPods go to e-waste/battery recycling, not the workbench.

    Earbud batteries are small but not harmless: a punctured button cell in a welded shell has nowhere to vent but into the plastic around it.

    Two storylines run through the AirPods record: a repairability score of zero, and a consumer gadget crossing into regulated medicine.

    Did you know

    • Left and right buds are different devices — different model numbers, different FCC IDs, and not interchangeable in pairing until re-mated as a set.
    • The case is a computer too: on Pro 2 it has its own radio, speaker, and firmware — a lost case is a lost device, not a lost accessory.
    • Your earbuds contain an FDA-authorized medical feature: the Pro 2’s hearing test and hearing aid functions run on the same H2 chip that plays music.
