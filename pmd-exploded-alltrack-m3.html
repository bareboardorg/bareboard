Exploded View: Amylior Alltrack M3

The Alltrack M3 is Amylior’s mid-wheel-drive complex-rehab chair: a Canadian-built power base (Vaudreuil-Dorion, Quebec) with a patented six-wheel Dual Action Suspension, PG Drives VR2 or R-net electronics, and a 300 lb capacity. It is also the rare chair whose maker publishes the full parts book openly, on its own website — every suspension arm, motor, wheel, and battery-box part, numbered. The catch is that the book is in French only. This page reproduces Section 3.1.3 of Amylior’s Livre de pièces — the M3 powered base in all five of its drive configurations — with every part number, plus the labeled overview from the owner’s manual.

Coverage: full IPB. The maker’s parts book for this base is complete and public: five exploded diagrams, 136 numbered parts. The maker’s descriptions are in French, as printed; the plain-English column next to them is ours. What the book does not cover — seating, electronics, the M3-HD — is named at the bottom of the page, not hidden.

The maker’s own warnings

Amylior prints these in the Alltrack M3 Owner’s Manual (OM | PD-A20601 | 1000896 | R03), Section VI, General Warnings. The wording below is the maker’s own, quoted word for word.

  • Read the warnings. “Read all warnings in this section. If you do not comply with these warnings, a fall, tip-over or loss of control may occur and cause severe injury to you or others.”
  • Get trained first. “Before using this power wheelchair, you should be trained in its safe use by your health care professional.”
  • Wear the positioning strap. “ALWAYS wear a positioning strap.”
  • Check the joystick every time. “DO NOT use your power wheelchair if the joystick does not spring back to the neutral position.” “DO NOT use your power wheelchair if the joystick rubber gaiter is ripped or damaged.”
  • Stay under the weight limit. “The user and items carried should NEVER exceed the total weight capacity identified on your power wheelchair.” Going over the limit is likely to damage the seat, the power seating options, the frame, or the fasteners, and may cause severe injury.
  • Do not modify the chair. “NEVER make any customizations, changes or modifications to any part of your Alltrack power wheelchair unless explicitly authorized by AMYLIOR. Doing so will void the warranty and may create a safety hazard.”
  • Freewheel and brake release levers. “DO NOT engage or disengage freewheel/brake release levers unless power to the wheelchair is off.” “Make sure both levers are completely engaged before turning power on to ensure proper control of the wheelchair.” With the levers disengaged the chair has no braking ability.
  • Turn it off when parked. “ALWAYS turn off all power to your wheelchair when you are parked, even for a moment.” That stops accidental movement from contact with the joystick and unintended movement from EMI sources.
  • Keep it out of water. “Your power wheelchair is not designed for use in a heavy rain storm, or in snowy or icy conditions.” “NEVER take your power wheelchair into a shower, tub, pool or sauna.”
  • Transport without the WC19 option. “NEVER allow anyone to sit in the wheelchair while it is secured in a moving vehicle” unless the chair has the WC19-ISO occupied-transport option. The built-in brackets are for securing an unoccupied chair only.

Quoted from the Owner’s Manual, Section VI (pp. 10–16).

How to read this page

The parts book is French only. No English edition of Amylior’s parts book was found in public. The Maker’s description column reproduces the book’s French text exactly as printed — those short labels are the part names, and they are what you quote to a parts desk. The What this part does column is our plain-English explanation of each part.

Colour codes. Parts whose number ends in -XXX* take a colour suffix: add the code for your chair’s colour to the part number (the book’s example: S-XXXX-BAO). The codes are PRISMA (noir Prismacolor, black), BAO (noir opale métallique, metallic opal black), BOR (orange brûlé métallique, metallic burnt orange), CBU (bleu bonbon, candy blue), CRE (rouge bonbon, candy red), GMC (gris charbon, charcoal grey), MLP (mauve minuit, midnight mauve), MWT (blanc mat, matte white), BLK (noir lustré, gloss black), MYG (vert militaire, military green), MYT (sable militaire, military sand), OPM (orange étincelant, sparkling orange), PKP (rose perlé, pearl pink), RZB (violine, violet), SBM (noir mat, matte black), SVM (argent métallisé, metallic silver).

Effective dates. The Effective column is the date from which that part applies, as printed in the book. Where a part was superseded, the book lists the newer number with its later date.

Which of the five sections is your chair? The book is organized by base generation and motor type. Generation 1 is the original M3 base; Generation 2 is the current base. The motor type is on the motor’s own label: SRG04 (gear release), SRG06 (brake release), SRG01 (brake release), or LiNX (brake release, with encoder). Gear-release motors have freewheel release levers; brake-release motors have brake release levers — the owner’s manual shows both (Section III, callouts 7 and 11).

Not illustrated. Rows marked “not illustrated” are parts the book lists in its tables with no callout on the diagram — hardware kits and pivot kits that are ordered by number alone.

Availability. Each page of the book carries a legend — COURANT (current), DISPONIBLE (available), DISCONTINUÉ (discontinued) — marking each part’s status. Those marks did not survive text extraction, so per-part status is not reproduced here; check the book itself before ordering.

Build order

The assemblies are laid out in the order you would build the chair: the whole chair first, then the powered base in its five drive configurations. Every step is one labeled figure from the maker’s guides, followed by its parts table. The Callout column is the number printed on the diagram.

  • The whole chair: Step 1: The Alltrack M3, labeled by Amylior
  • The powered base, Generation 1: Step 2: Mid-wheel drive, Generation 1, gear-release motor SRG04

Stage 1 — The whole chair

Start with the chair itself. The owner’s manual labels the M3 once, with fifteen numbered parts, and that is the map for everything that follows: the seat and its frame sit on the powered base, the drive wheels sit in the middle, and the casters sit at the front and back.

The chair in numbers, from the manual’s own tables

All of these come from the Owner’s Manual’s Product Specifications (Section XIII, pp. 46–47) and its description of the chair (Section III, p. 5). Where the manual gives a figure for the M3-HD as well, it is marked HD.

  • Maximum user weight: 300 lb (136 kg) for the M3; 450 lb (205 kg) for the M3-HD; 400 lb (181 kg) for the elevating seat on the M3-HD.
  • Maximum speed: 6.5 mph (10 km/h) standard; 5.4 mph (9 km/h) as an option on the M3-HD.
  • Range (theoretical, without recharging): Group 24 batteries: 42 miles (68.5 km); Group 22 batteries: 30.5 miles (49.2 km).
  • Batteries: two 12-volt deep-cycle AGM or Gel batteries, Group 22NF (50 Ah) or Group 24 (70 Ah); 8-amp off-board charger. (The manual’s specification table misprints “AGM” as “AMG”.) The battery lesson is on this site.
  • Electronics: PG Drives VR2 (non-expandable, 90 A) or R-net (expandable, 90 A or 120 A).
  • Suspension: patented Dual Action Suspension — fully independent six-wheel interactive suspension with vertical shock absorption.
  • Base size: base length 35.125 in (890 mm); base width 23.375 in (595 mm) with Group 22 batteries, 25.375 in (645 mm) with Group 24; the narrow N2221 base is 21 in (535 mm) wide, the N2424 is 23.375 in.
  • Base turning radius: 20 in (510 mm).
  • Overall length with legrest: 46 in (1170 mm). Overall height (floor to top of back canes): 31.5–46.25 in (800–1175 mm).
  • Ground clearance: 3.5 in (90 mm) with 14-inch drive wheels; 2.875 in (75 mm) with 12.5-inch drive wheels.
  • Stability: static stability 20° downhill, 20° uphill, 10° sideways; dynamic stability uphill 10° (8° for the N2221 narrow base).
  • Climbable obstacle: Group 24: 2.75 in (70 mm); Group 22: 2.95 in (75 mm); N2221: 2.95 in (75 mm).
  • Seat-to-floor height: 15.5–18.75 in (395–475 mm) with power tilt or seat elevate; 17.5–18.25 in (445–465 mm) with combined tilt and elevate. Seat elevate: 11 in (280 mm); driving while elevated runs at 25% of maximum speed.
  • Wheels: drive wheels 12.5 or 14 inches; casters 6 or 8 inches.
  • Weight: base 126 lb (57 kg) for the N2221, 128 lb (58 kg) for the 23.375-inch base, 133 lb (60 kg) for the 25.375-inch base; batteries 2 × 36.6 lb (16.6 kg) for Group 22NF, 2 × 54 lb (24.5 kg) for Group 24.
  • Operating conditions: −13°F to +122°F (−25°C to +50°C), dry heat. Storage: −40°F to +149°F (−40°C to +65°C), charge monthly.
  • Service life: five years, per the manual.
  • Class: Class B. US codes (HCPCS): K0848 / K0856 / K0861 (M3); K0850 / K0858 / K0862 (M3-HD).

If the chair will not move, the manual’s troubleshooting guide for power seating is on p. 40 and its maintenance chapter is Section XI (pp. 43–45) — and the VR2 and R-net fault codes are translated on this site: VR2 fault codes · R-net fault codes.

What the parts book covers, and where it stops

This page reproduces Section 3.1.3 of the parts book only — the M3 powered base. The same book covers the rest of the Alltrack family in other sections: the Alltrack P (§3.1.1), P R (§3.1.2), R (§3.1.5), R HD (§3.1.6), HD (§3.1.7), and the M3-HD (§3.1.4) each have their own base sections, and the covers for all mid-wheel-drive Generation 2 chairs are in §3.1.8 and the tires in §3.1.9. Seating systems and electronics (joysticks, power modules, harnesses) live in other sections of the full parts book, which this file — Section 3, motorized bases only — does not contain.

No separate Amylior service manual for the Alltrack M3 was found in public; the parts book is the service document. The owner’s manual points to Amylior’s Instruction Guides (on amylior.com, under Support / For Pros) for adjustments, maintenance, and repairs.

The record on this chair

The Alltrack series was cleared by the FDA on 9 October 2009 as the AMYPOWER ALL TRACK SERIES POWER WHEELCHAIR (510(k) K092225), a Class II powered wheelchair (21 CFR 890.3860, product code ITI), substantially equivalent to the Invacare Storm TDX (K023589) and the Invacare Storm (K940051). Its stated intended use is mobility for persons limited to a sitting position. The clearance covers the Alltrack series; the M3 is the mid-wheel-drive member of that series, sold in the US under HCPCS codes K0848, K0856, and K0861.

No recall for Amylior or the Alltrack appears in the FDA enforcement database (checked 2026-11-17).

What is missing, in the open

An English parts book. Amylior publishes this parts book in French only, and no English edition was found in public. Every description on this page is therefore the book’s French text, with our English explanation beside it. If Amylior publishes an English edition, this page gains it.

The M3-HD. The heavy-duty M3-HD has its own section of the same book (§3.1.4) and its own base configurations; it is not covered here. The owner’s manual covers both chairs, and the specifications above mark the HD figures where they differ.

Per-part availability. The book marks each part current, available, or discontinued, but those marks could not be reproduced from the document’s text layer. Check the book itself before ordering.

The serial number. The owner’s manual (Section I) shows the serial-number label on the back of the base, under the seat. The first two digits of the serial number are the last two digits of the year of production. That label is the one key you hold: every part on this chair is orderable by a dealer using it — and now you have the numbers to check their work.
