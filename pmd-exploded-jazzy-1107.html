    Exploded View: Pride Jazzy 1107

    This page is different, and the difference is the story. Pride Mobility does not publish an Illustrated Parts Breakdown for the Jazzy 1107 — not for owners, not openly at all. What Pride does publish is the labeled overview in the owner’s manual — the whole chair, the power base, the electrical parts, the seat adjustments, the drive wheel taken apart, and the batteries, each with its own diagram — plus a four-page troubleshooting sheet that names the fault codes for both controllers the chair was sold with. This page puts both together, and names the gap exactly: everything below the manual — motors, gearboxes, casters, shrouds, harnesses — has no public part number, because Pride sells those parts through its dealers.

    Coverage: manual-only. The Jazzy 1107 is a mid-wheel-drive power chair: two motors, Active-Trac suspension, and two 12-volt U-1 batteries. Mid-wheel drive means the powered wheels sit in the middle of the chair, with a small swivelling caster wheel at the back and an anti-tip wheel at the front — that is why it turns in a tight circle. The controller is the electronics that drive the chair: the joystick you hold and the power module inside the base that connects it to the motors and batteries. This chair was sold with one of two controllers: Pride’s own FLIGHT, shown in the manual’s electrical diagram, or a PG Drives VSI. U-1 is a battery size standard — the size, not a brand. Weight capacity is 300 lb (136 kg). The chair is 23.375 inches wide and, by the manual’s own drawing, turns in a 21.875-inch radius.

    Not the Quantum 1107. Pride also sold the same base as the Quantum 1107, with its own manual (INFMANU2980, 2008) under the Quantum Rehab brand. The chairs share the base and most of the diagrams; the branding and the manual are different. That chair is not covered here.

The closest public documents are the two above. If Pride publishes an 1107 breakdown, this page gets the full exploded treatment the same week.

    Stage 1 — The chair and the base

    Start with the two halves. The chair has two main assemblies: the seat assembly and the power base assembly. The seat assembly is the armrests, seatback, and seat base. The power base assembly is two motor/brake assemblies, two drive wheels, two anti-tip wheels, two castor wheels, two battery boxes, and wiring harnesses. A caster wheel is a small wheel that is not powered; it swivels so the chair can turn. An anti-tip wheel is a small wheel at the front that stops the chair tipping forward. A harness is a bundle of wires with plugs on the ends.

    The power base is the whole lower half of the chair. The two battery boxes sit on top of it under covers, the motors sit low on each side, and the seat locks onto the trapeze bars across the top. A trapeze bar is one of the bars across the top of the base that the seat slides onto.

    Step 3 — Electrical parts

    The electrical parts live on or inside the power base. The controller power module sits inside the base, and the main circuit breaker is on the rear battery box. The power module is the box that connects the joystick to the motors, the batteries, and the off-board charger. The main circuit breaker is a safety switch: when the batteries and the motors are heavily strained, it trips to prevent damage. If it trips, let the chair rest for about a minute, push the breaker button back in, turn the controller on, and carry on. If it keeps tripping, call your Pride Dealer. The diagram shows the FLIGHT controller.

    Stage 2 — Freewheel levers

    Each motor has a manual freewheel lever on top of it. The levers let you disconnect the drive motors from the gearboxes so the chair can be pushed by hand. Pull both levers up to engage the motors (drive mode); push both levers down to disengage them (freewheel mode). If a lever is hard to move, rock the chair gently back and forth and try again. Never use the lever handles as tie-down points. And remember the warning above: in freewheel mode the brakes are off, so only ever do this on level ground with an attendant present.

    Step 4 — The freewheel levers

    Stage 3 — Assembly and disassembly

    The chair comes apart for transport without tools: the seat lifts off, the foot platform lifts off, the two battery boxes slide off, and the base splits into three sections. One rule from the manual: any nylon insert lock nut you remove must be replaced with a new one — never reuse it, because the nylon insert is damaged by removal and the nut will not hold as tightly.

Before the first installation, the rear seat interface has to be fitted to the seat base: slide it into the rear extrusion, then squeeze the retaining pin and clip it onto the interface.

    Step 7 — Seat installation

    Slide the seat onto the side trapeze bars and slowly lower it onto the front trapeze bar until it locks into place, then flip the seat latch safety down. Never pick the seat frame up by the armrests — they pivot freely and you can lose control of the seat. The controller cable is the fragile part of this job: route it so it cannot be pinched in the seat hinge, under the armrest or toward the inside of the armrest pad — never on the outside — and tie it down so it cannot catch in the drive tyres, get pinched in the seat frame, or snag in a doorway.

    Step 8 — Power base components

    The power base splits into six parts for transport and storage: the foot platform, the front and rear battery boxes, and the left, right, and centre frame sections. To take it apart: turn the controller off, disconnect it, remove the seat, press the foot platform release button and lift the foot platform off, flip down the battery box latches and slide the battery boxes back and off, flip up the centre section latch, rotate the centre section forward, and lift each side section up and off by its side frame handle. Put it back together in reverse order — and when you do, make sure the battery box latches are up (locked) and the centre section latch is down (locked).

    Stage 4 — The seat

    The seat adjusts four ways: height and angle, position, seatback angle, and armrest width, angle, and height. The job needs a metric/standard socket set and ratchet, an adjustable spanner, and thread lock. Thread lock is a liquid that stops screws working loose. If your chair was configured by your Pride Dealer, check with your healthcare professional before changing the seat position or making any other adjustment — some adjustments change the chair’s centre of gravity. Remove the occupant from the chair before making any adjustment.

    Step 10 — Seat height

    You can change the seat height to one of two positions in 2.5 cm (1 in.) steps by raising the front seat interface and the side frame handles. Raise or lower only the front seat interface or the side frame handles and you also change the seat base angle — the dump, the tilt of the seat. Turn the controller off, disconnect it, remove the seat, loosen the screws that attach the side frame handles and front seat interface to the seat posts, pull the retaining clips, move the posts up or down, put the clips back, and refit each screw with fresh thread lock.

    Part (maker’s label)What this part does
    Front Seat InterfaceThe bar at the front of the seat mounting; raising or lowering it changes the seat height.
    Side Frame HandlesThe handles on the side frame sections; raising or lowering them changes the seat height. They are also what you grab to lift a side section off the base.
    Seat PostThe upright the seat interface and side frame handles bolt to; it slides up or down to set the height.
    Retaining ClipsThe clips that hold the seat posts to the power base; pull them out to move the posts.

    Step 11 — Seat position

    You can move the seat forward or rearward by changing the extrusion mounting position: turn the controller off, unplug it, remove the seat, take both extrusions off the bottom of the seat, and refasten them on a different set of mounting holes. You must move both extrusions the same number of holes, either forward or rearward.

    Part (maker’s label)What this part does
    Seat Mounting HolesThe row of holes under the seat; moving both extrusions the same number of holes moves the seat forward or back.

    Step 12 — Seatback and armrests

    If your chair has a reclining seat, pull up on the seatback release lever on the right side of the seat base, lean to the angle you want, and release the lever. If it has an adjustable seatback, it sets to four angles: 90°, 102°, 105°, or 107° — remove the adjusting screw from each seat hinge, set the back, and refit the screws. Each armrest’s width changes independently — loosen the two knobs on each side of the armrest receiver bracket, slide the armrest in or out, and tighten. Changing the armrest width may increase the overall width of your chair. To change the armrest angle, lift the armrest straight up, loosen the locking nut, turn the adjusting screw to raise or lower the front of the armrest, and tighten the nut.

    Part (maker’s label)What this part does
    Locking NutThe nut that locks the armrest angle adjusting screw once it is set.
    Armrest Angle Adjusting ScrewTurn it to raise or lower the front of the armrest.
    Armrest Height Adjusting SetscrewsLoosen them to raise or lower the armrest, then tighten to hold it.
    Seatback Angle Adjusting ScrewThe screw at each seat hinge; remove it, set the back to 90°, 102°, 105°, or 107°, and refit it.

    Step 13 — Under the armrest

    You can move the controller in toward or out away from the armrest, or move it to the other armrest for left-hand or right-hand use. Flip the armrest up so it is perpendicular to the floor, loosen the setscrew on the controller bracket, slide the controller to the position you want, and tighten the setscrew. To change sides: turn the controller off, unplug it, cut the wire ties, slide it out, and fit it into the other armrest. Do not place the controller harness where it can be pinched in the seat frame or the power base frame.

    Part (maker’s label)What this part does
    SetscrewThe screw on the controller bracket under the armrest; loosen it to slide the joystick in or out, tighten it to lock the position.

    Step 14 — The foot platform

    The foot platform adjusts three ways. Height: remove the quick release fasteners from the foot platform bracket, raise or lower the platform in 1.27 cm (0.50 in.) steps, refit the fasteners, and tighten. Depth: remove the fasteners, move the platform in or out, and refit them. Angle: flip the platform up, find the adjustment screw underneath, and turn it — see Step 16.

    Part (maker’s label)What this part does
    Foot Platform BracketThe bracket the foot platform bolts to; remove its fasteners to change the height or depth.
    Quick Release FastenersThe two fasteners that hold the foot platform to the bracket; each is a bolt, a lever, and a nut — see Step 15.

    Step 15 — The quick release fastener

    Each quick release fastener is a bolt, a lever, and a nut. The lever has a cam on the end that lets it clamp into place: lever open means unclamped, lever closed means clamped. To clamp one: make sure the lever is open, turn the nut clockwise until it is snug, then rotate the lever to the fully closed position. If the lever will not rotate fully closed, turn the nut anticlockwise one-quarter or one-half turn and try again.

    Step 16 — Under the foot platform

    You can adjust the angle of the foot platform with a hex key. Find the setscrew on the underside of the foot platform and turn it anticlockwise to raise or lower the front of the platform.

    Part (maker’s label)What this part does

    Step 17 — The anti-tip wheel assembly

    The anti-tip wheels give the chair extra stability on rough surfaces. They come preset from the factory for smooth surfaces or indoor use only. If you plan on using the chair on rough surfaces, they may need adjusting — if the chair tips forward too much when coming to a stop, or if the anti-tip wheels constantly rub the ground. Each hole is 1.27 cm (0.50 in.) apart: loosen nut A, remove bolt B, raise or lower the wheel, refit bolt B, tighten nut A, and set the other anti-tip wheel to the same height. Pride says to consult your authorised Pride Dealer before changing the anti-tip wheel height, and never to remove the anti-tip wheels. Each drive tyre must be inflated to 2.4 bar (35 psi), and you must be seated in the chair to adjust the anti-tip wheels properly.

    Step 18 — The positioning belt

    Your chair may have a positioning belt that adjusts for operator comfort. It supports you so you do not slide down or forward in the seat. It is not a restraining device. To fit it: remove the rearmost screw that holds each seat hinge to the seat base, put the screw through the supplied washer and the belt and back into the seat base, and tighten both screws. To wear it: once seated, push the metal tab on one side into the plastic housing on the other side until you hear a click, then pull the excess strap until it is secure but not uncomfortable. To release it, press the push button on the plastic housing. Make sure the belt is properly secured and adjusted before each use, and inspect it for loose or damaged parts before each use.

    Part (maker’s label)What this part does
    Metal TabThe tab on one side of the belt; push it into the plastic housing until it clicks. Named from the manual’s own text.
    Plastic HousingThe housing on the other side of the belt that the metal tab clicks into; press its push button to release the belt. Named from the manual’s own text.
    Push Button MechanismThe button on the plastic housing; press it to release the belt. Named from the manual’s own text.

    Stage 5 — Charging, wheels, and batteries

    The chair uses two long-lasting, 12-volt, deep-cycle batteries. Deep-cycle means the battery is built to be drained down and recharged over and over, unlike a car battery. They are sealed and maintenance free — there is no fluid level to check. AGM (absorbed glass mat) and Gel-Cell are the two sealed deep-cycle types Pride accepts; both are sealed and maintenance-free. Never use a car battery charger — use only the supplied 5-amp off-board charger.

    Step 19 — Battery boxes

    To charge: position the front of the chair next to a standard electrical outlet, switch the controller off, plug the off-board charger into the charger/programming socket on the controller, then plug the charger into the wall. When the batteries are fully charged, unplug the charger from the wall first, then from the controller. Charge 8 to 14 hours after daily use. If you use the chair once a week or less, charge at least once a week for 12 to 14 hours. Never charge for more than 24 hours in one charging cycle. The charger takes the wall voltage of 120 VAC (alternating current) and converts it to 24 VDC (direct current) for the batteries. You can also take both battery boxes off the base and charge them through the off-board charger port on the front battery box — but the boxes must be connected to each other to charge. The charger will not work at all once the batteries have dropped to nearly zero voltage; if that happens, call your Pride Dealer. New batteries need breaking in: fully recharge before first use, run the chair gently, recharge for 8 to 14 hours, and repeat — after four or five charging cycles they reach 100%.

    Part (maker’s label)What this part does
    Off-Board Charger PortThe socket on the front battery box; plug the off-board charger in here to charge the boxes off the chair.
    Battery BoxesThe two boxes, front and rear, each holding one 12-volt battery; the charger port is on the front one. Named from the manual’s own text.

    Step 20 — The drive wheel, apart

    If you have pneumatic tyres and you have a flat, you can replace the tube. If your chair has a solid tyre insert, you must replace the entire wheel assembly. The wheels should only be serviced or replaced by an authorised Pride Dealer or a qualified technician. The steps, from the manual: turn the controller off, put the chair up on blocks, and if you are changing a pneumatic tyre, completely deflate it before removing the wheel. Remove the drive wheel nut and washer from the axle, pull the wheel off, remove the nuts and washers from the wheel hub, and separate the front and rear rim. Replace the tube, or the whole assembly if it is a solid tyre. Reassemble the rims, refit the nuts and washers, slide the wheel back onto the axle — making sure the axle key is in the axle slot — and refit the nut and washer and tighten them. Inflate pneumatic tyres to 2.4 bar (35 psi), and take the chair off the blocks.

    Axle SlotThe slot in the axle the key sits in.
    Drive Wheel WasherThe washer under the drive wheel nut.
    Drive Wheel NutThe nut that holds the drive wheel on the axle; remove only this nut and washer to take the wheel off.
    TyreThe rubber tyre on the drive wheel; pneumatic (air-filled, with a tube) or solid, depending on your chair.
    TubeThe inner tube inside a pneumatic tyre; replace it if the tyre goes flat.

    Step 21 — Battery boxes, inside

    To replace the batteries: turn the controller off, make sure the chair is in drive mode, remove the battery boxes, disconnect the wiring harness from the batteries, and lift the batteries out of the boxes. Put the new batteries in, reconnect the harnesses following the wiring diagram on the decal — red wire to the red positive (+) terminal, black wire to the black negative (−) terminal — and tighten the fasteners so the connections are secure. Put the battery boxes back on the base with the latches up (locked), and charge the batteries. The batteries sit flat in the boxes with their terminals facing inward, toward each other.     Faults and troubleshooting

    The owner’s manual itself has no fault-code table — its troubleshooting is a short list of symptoms and one corrective-maintenance check. Pride’s own troubleshooting sheet, INFINFB2041, fills the gap: it names the fault codes for both controllers this chair was sold with. Everything below is from that sheet, in our own words, with the sheet’s own fixes. A fault code is an allegation by the controller, not a diagnosis — it says where to look.

    If the chair seems dead (the controller does not light up when you press the on/off key): check the batteries are wired properly against the wiring diagram on the power base, the battery terminal connections are tight and secure, the battery boxes are fully connected to each other and to the chair, the main circuit breaker has not tripped (push the reset button in), and the controller is fully connected to the power base.

    If the range per charge is shrinking: check whether the batteries have ever been replaced (batteries over a year old may be defective), how often you charge against how often you use the chair (daily use means charging every day for 8–14 hours; even an unused chair needs charging once a week for 12–14 hours), and whether the charger lights show the batteries are actually receiving a charge. Continual undercharging shortens battery life, and letting the battery meter fall into the red again and again does the same.

    If the chair runs slower than it used to: check when the batteries were last charged (low charge means less power to the motors), and the temperature — if the electrical system overheats it protects itself by cutting power, and the chair gradually slows to a stop. Let it cool down and it resumes normal operation.

    If the chair pulls to one side: check the joystick is being pushed straight and not at an angle, both freewheel levers are in the drive position (one lever in freewheel and the chair veers severely), both tyres are at 35 psi (2.4 bar) if pneumatic (the chair pulls toward the underinflated side), and both castor wheels pivot freely.

    If the chair powers up but will not move: check both freewheel levers are not in the freewheel position, then read the fault code on the battery condition meter. The chair has either a VSI or a FLIGHT controller, and each flashes its own codes.

VSI speed LEDs scrolling: the VSI is in lock mode. To unlock, push the joystick fully forward until it beeps, then fully rearward until it beeps, then return it to the neutral position.

    FLIGHT diagnostic flash codes

    On a FLIGHT controller, the battery condition meter is ten lights in an arc over the on/off key. All ten lit means full charge; as the voltage drops the lights go out from right to left. When the controller detects a fault, the lights flash in a numbered sequence followed by a 2-second pause. From INFINFB2041:

    FLIGHT left red battery light flashing: the battery voltage is very low — charge the batteries immediately. FLIGHT lights scrolling from right to left and the chair will not drive: the FLIGHT is in lock mode — press the horn button twice to unlock. FLIGHT lights scrolling left to right, alternating with a steady display: the FLIGHT is plugged in for charging; the steady display shows the current state of charge. FLIGHT all battery lights flashing rapidly nonstop and the chair will not drive: the joystick was not in the neutral (centre) position when the controller was turned on (an Out Of Neutral At Power Up). Release the joystick to neutral and restart the chair.
