    Exploded View: Pride Jazzy 1450

    This page is the full breakdown. Pride Mobility publishes a complete Illustrated Parts Breakdown for the Jazzy 1450 — INFMANU5144 Rev. A, 113 pages — and this page works through it for the VR2 chair: the frame, the suspension, the motors, the wheels, the electronics, the joystick, the shrouds, the batteries, and the charger, each with the maker’s own part numbers. One thing to know up front: the breakdown Pride published is the Australian export, for chairs whose serial numbers start JC4. The part numbers are Pride’s own global system and the chair is the same chair — but the charger in the Australian book comes with an Australian plug. The US chair ships with the US cordset, and the same parts list names it.

    Coverage: full IPB — VR2 configuration. The Jazzy 1450 is a bariatric power chair — bariatric means built for a heavier user, 600 lb here — with front-wheel drive: the big powered wheels sit at the front of the chair, which is what lets it turn in tight indoor spaces. It runs on a 90-amp PG VR2 controller — PG is Penny & Giles, the company that makes the controller, and VR2 is the model — and two 12-volt Group 24 batteries. Group 24 is a battery size standard, the size, not a brand. Top speed is up to 4.9 mph and range up to 16.5 miles per charge, on Pride’s current specifications sheet.

    The same book also covers the Quantum 1450, the sister chair, which can be ordered with Q-Logic electronics instead of VR2. Q-Logic is Quantum Rehab’s own controller family. This page follows the Jazzy 1450 with VR2 — the standard Jazzy configuration, models 1450-2VHD and 1450-2SPVHD, Medicare codes K0826, K0827, and K0839. The Q-Logic assemblies are in the library copy of the book if you need them.

    One naming wrinkle, stated in the book itself: the motors listed for the VR2 chair are Hammer II motors, while the electronics assembly is labeled for Hammer XL motors. The Hammer II replaced the Hammer XL, from serial number JC528113047020 for the Jazzy 1450 (JC427313082020 for the Quantum 1450) onward. If your chair is older than that cutoff and a motor must be replaced, both motors must be replaced together.

    Stage 1 — The chair as Pride labels it

    The chair has two main assemblies: the seat assembly and the power base assembly. The seat assembly is the armrests, seatback, and seat base. The power base assembly is two motor/brake assemblies, two drive wheels, two caster wheels, two anti-tip wheels, two batteries, and the wiring harnesses. An anti-tip wheel is a small wheel at the front that stops the chair tipping forward on a slope. A caster wheel is a small wheel that is not powered; it swivels so the chair can turn.

    Step 2 — Electrical components

    The electrical parts live on the power base. The controller power module is the box that lets the joystick talk to the batteries and the motors. The main circuit breaker sits in front of the batteries on top of the main frame: it is a safety switch that trips — cuts the power — when the batteries and motors are heavily strained, for example by an excessive load. Unlike some chairs, this breaker resets itself: let the chair rest for about a minute, turn the controller on, and carry on. If it keeps tripping, call your Quantum Rehab Provider.

    Step 3 — The freewheel levers

    Each motor has a manual freewheel lever. The levers let you disconnect the drive motors from the gearboxes so the chair can be pushed by hand. Push both levers down for freewheel mode (motors disconnected); pull both levers up for drive mode (motors engaged). Turn the power off before you move the levers, or the controller will show an error code. And remember the warning above: in freewheel mode the brakes are off, so only ever do this on level ground.

    Part (maker’s label)What this part does
    Manual Freewheel LeverOne on each motor. Down means the motors are disconnected and the chair rolls freely — with no brakes. Up means the motors drive the chair.

    Step 5 — Motor mounts, anti-tips, and suspension

    The motors bolt to the frame through these mounts, and the mounts carry the front suspension. Suspension is the spring system that lets the wheels ride over bumps while the seat stays level. This chair’s suspension is a swing-arm design: each side swings on an arm, with a coil spring — a spring rated at 1,000 pounds per inch — taking the load. The anti-tip wheels bolt to the mounts too.

    Step 7 — The Hammer II VR2 motors

    Two motors drive this chair, one per front wheel. Each is a motor and gearbox in one unit: a 3,700-RPM motor geared down 32.79 to 1, so the wheel shaft turns at 114 RPM. RPM means revolutions per minute. The gearbox has a tapered shaft — a shaft that narrows toward the end so the wheel hub wedges on tight — and an electromechanical brake that releases when the chair drives and locks when it stops. The motors use PG connectors, the plug type for the PG VR2 controller. The complete motor does not include the motor mounting hardware or the wheel mounting hardware: those are callouts D1 and C1 below, ordered separately. And the serial-number rule from the book: the Hammer II replaced the Hammer XL from Jazzy 1450 serial number JC528113047020 onward; on an older chair, replace both motors together.

    Stage 3 — Electronics

    The electronics are the controller, the cables, and the breaker that protect them. This section follows the VR2 configuration of the Jazzy 1450: a 90-amp VR2 controller with the Hammer XL program. The book also lists the same electronics in two other VR2 forms — with a tilt switch and with a joystick-driven tilt — for chairs ordered with power positioning; those are in the library copy of the book.

    Step 10 — VR2 electronics

    The parent assembly (A1–B9) is the whole electronics package in one orderable number. Inside it, the controller (A1) is the 90-amp VR2 unit, and the partial assembly (B1–B9) is everything except the controller. The battery cables (B2) carry a 125-amp, 32-volt fusible link — a short length of wire designed to melt and break the circuit if the current ever gets dangerously high. The circuit breaker (B7) is a 100-amp self-resetting breaker: it trips under heavy strain and resets itself.

The book details the MK gel battery assembly here; it also lists Universal and Interceptor battery assemblies, which are in the library copy of the book.

    Step 14 — The charger

    The charger is an 8-amp off-board unit — off-board means it sits outside the chair and plugs in, rather than being built into the chair. It takes 100 to 240 volts AC from the wall and puts out 28.6 to 29.6 volts DC at 8 amps, and it can be switched between AGM and gel-cell batteries. Low inhibit means the charger tells the chair not to drive while it is plugged in. The charger itself (A1) comes with the USA cordset; the other rows are the alternate power cords for other countries — the Australian cord is E1, which is the one the Australian book’s chair would have shipped with.

    Step 15 — Battery installation

    To change a battery: turn the controller off, make sure the chair is in drive mode, loosen the yellow thumbscrews and remove the rear cover, disconnect the quick-release battery connectors, and lift the batteries out. The wiring rule is printed on the chair and in the manual: red wire to the red positive (+) terminal, black wire to the black negative (−) terminal. Never mix an old battery with a new one — replace both at the same time. Each battery weighs 52 lb: get help lifting them.

    Charging: use only the supplied 8-amp off-board charger — never a car battery charger. Charge 8 to 14 hours after daily use, plus 4 more hours after the charger says it is done, and give the batteries one continuous 48-hour charge once a month. If you use the chair once a week or less, charge at least once a week for at least 24 hours. If the charger’s lights do not show charging complete within 24 hours, call your Quantum Rehab Provider.

    The full record

    Recalls. No FDA recall names the Jazzy 1450 (OpenFDA recall database, checked 20 November 2026).

    Clearance. No 510(k) premarket notification was found naming the Jazzy 1450 as the device (FDA 510(k) database via OpenFDA, checked 20 November 2026). The 510(k) numbers in the Jazzy family name other chairs: K042612 names the Jazzy 600, cleared 6 October 2004, and K945936 names the original Jazzy. Neither is this chair’s clearance, and this page claims neither. The public record does not show a separate Jazzy 1450 clearance.

    Adverse-event reports. No MAUDE adverse-event report was found for a device named Jazzy 1450 (OpenFDA, checked 20 November 2026). An adverse-event report, when one exists, is an allegation, not a finding.

    Changeover. The Hammer II motor replaced the Hammer XL motor from Jazzy 1450 serial number JC528113047020 onward (Quantum 1450: JC427313082020). The serial-number cutoff is printed in the parts breakdown itself, and it matters when ordering motors: on a chair older than the cutoff, both motors must be replaced together.
