Contribute — your first autopsy report in 30 minutes

You do not need to be an expert. You need a retired device, a screwdriver, a phone camera, and honesty about what you don't know. This page is written so anyone can do it.

What you need

  • A retired device you legally own (surplus, auction, your own drawer).
  • A screwdriver set, a multimeter if you have one, and a phone camera.
  • A plain text editor (any one — even Notepad).
  • The template: templates/autopsy-report.md

Six steps

  1. Pick a device. Start small: a router, a remote, a dead laptop. Not a life-critical or civic device, and not one still in service.
  2. Photograph everything before you touch it. The label, the screws, the sealed case. These are your "before" evidence.
  3. Open it slowly. Photograph the board. Note every chip marking — even if you don't know what it is yet. Markings are how you find datasheets (see the FoundryErasRare ManualsGlossary).
  4. Find the public documents. Search the chip markings on the vendor's site and distributors. Preserve what you find — that's a Library contribution too.
  5. Write the report. Fill the template. Every finding gets a photo or measurement. Everything you're unsure of gets "unknown — dated." That's not weakness; that's the brand.
  6. Send it. Open a pull request against the site repo, or email it. An editor will check the evidence, not your credentials.

Other ways to contribute

  • Score a Hot 100 entry — find a cited, dated figure for units in circulation.
  • Nominate a Most Wanted device — device, hiding place, why it matters, legal path.
  • Audit a tool for the Watchmen board — nmap it, hash its firmware.
  • Preserve a document — a public datasheet about to disappear. Download, hash, add to the Library.
  • Fix our words — if a page is too hard to read, that's a bug. Tell us.
The only hard rules: retired and legal devices only; verified findings only; no leaked or NDA material; no circumvention how-tos. Everything else is negotiable.
