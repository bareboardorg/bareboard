The Autopsy Report

An autopsy report is what BareBoard does best: we take a device, open it, and publish what is actually inside — verified, dated, and honest about what we don't know.

The three rules

  1. Every claim has evidence. A photo, a measurement, a screenshot, a citation.
  2. Every unknown is marked. Write "unknown — dated 2026-10-08" rather than guessing.
  3. The device was legally acquired and retired. Never an in-service, life-critical, or civic device.

Required fields

FieldWhat goes in it
Device / model / yearExact model number and year. Photos of the label.
AcquisitionWhere you got it, when, and that it was legal (receipt, auction lot, surplus).
Tools usedScrewdrivers, multimeter, programmer — and whether each tool was audited (watchmen).
FindingsNumbered. Each finding has its evidence attached.
UncertaintyWhat you could not verify, dated and marked.
Disclosure statusVendor contacted? CVD/ICS-CERT filed? Coordinated timeline.
SourcesEvery public source used, with dates.
Anyone can do this. The full template is in templates/autopsy-report.md, and Contribute walks you through your first report in 30 minutes.

BareBoard Legal Framework & Declarations

  • No AI training: All text, findings, and original photography on this site are copyrighted. Automated scraping for AI model training is prohibited. Crawlers that ignore robots.txt are trespassing.
  • Educational nature: BareBoard operates exclusively as a non-commercial, open educational resource. All material is compiled for academic evaluation, hardware preservation, and technical literacy under 17 U.S.C. § 107 (fair use).
  • Zero-leak / no-binary clause: This site does not host, distribute, or mirror proprietary firmware binaries, encrypted operating system keys, or weaponizable exploits. Documentation covers only the public, physical realities of circuit hardware.
  • Property verification: Every hardware autopsy is performed on personally owned, legally acquired equipment. No rented or third-party infrastructure is used.
  • Public source sourcing: Comparative and regulatory data is aggregated from official public filings, including the U.S. Federal Communications Commission equipment authorization database.
